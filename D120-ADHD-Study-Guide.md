## 🧠 MASTER MNEMONIC LIST

### **Vaginitis: The "pH Test"**
- **pH < 4.5** = **C**andida = **C**ottage cheese = **C**leans up with **F**luconazole
- **pH > 4.5** = **B**V or **T**rich
  - **B**V = **B**ad smell (fishy) = **B**ring Metronidazole
  - **T**rich = **T**wo people (treat both) = **T**urban-green frothy

### **BV vs Trich vs Candida: "The Disco Club"**
**BV** (Bacterial Vaginosis) - The **GRAY** guy with **FISHY** cologne  
- **G**ray homogeneous discharge
- **F**ishy odor (whiff test +)
- **C**lue cells on wet mount
- pH **>4.5**
- Rx: **Metro**politan (metronidazole)

**TRICH** (Trichomonas) - The **GREEN** party animal  
- **F**rothy **G**reen-yellow discharge
- **S**TI - treat **BOTH** partners
- **S**trawberry cervix
- pH **>4.5**
- Rx: **Metro** 2g × 1 (both partners)

**CANDIDA** - The **WHITE** cottage cheese platter  
- **T**hick **W**hite discharge
- **P**ruritus (itching)
- **H**yphae on KOH prep
- pH **<4.5**
- Rx: **F**luconazole 150mg × 1

### **STI Ulcers: "SOFT vs HARD"**
**SOFT** = **PAINFUL**  
- **S**oft chancre = **H**aemophilus ducreyi (Chancroid)
- **H**erpes **S**implex = vesicles → ulcers

**HARD** = **PAINLESS**  
- **H**ard chancre = Syphilis (Treponema pallidum)
- Rx: Penicillin G benzathine 2.4M units IM × 1

### **Syphilis Stages: "1-2-3"**
1. **Primary** = **1** painless chancre (heals in 3-6 weeks)
2. **Secondary** = **2** body parts (palms + soles rash, condyloma lata)
3. **Tertiary** = **3** systems (cardiovascular, neuro, gummas)

### **PID Minimum Criteria: "CAU for PID"**
**C**ervical motion tenderness, OR  
**A**dnexal tenderness, OR  
**U**terine tenderness  
= **PID** (start empiric treatment)

### **Pregnancy Bleeding: "PAIN tells the story"**
- **PAINLESS** = Placenta previa (NO digital exam!)
- **PAINFUL** = Abruptio placentae (rigid abdomen)

### **Preeclampsia Severe Features: "HELP-T"**
- **H**eadache (visual changes)
- **E**levated liver enzymes (>2× normal)
- **L**ow platelets (<100,000)
- **P**ulmonary edema
- **T**hrombocytopenia (included in "L")

**HELLP Syndrome** = **H**emolysis + **E**levated **L**iver enzymes + **L**ow **P**latelets

### **BPH Treatment: "Alpha comes FIRST"**
- **Alpha-blockers** (tamsulosin) = **FIRST**-line (fast symptom relief)
- **5-Alpha Reductase Inhibitors** (finasteride) = **SECOND** (shrinks prostate, takes months)

### **Testicular Emergency: "6-12-24 Rule"**
Testicular torsion salvage rates:
- **<6 hours** = ~**100%**
- **6-12 hours** = ~**50%**
- **>24 hours** = ~**10%**
**→ NEVER delay for imaging!**

### **PrEP Labs: "HIV-3-HBC"**
Before starting PrEP:
- **HIV** test (must be negative)
- **3** month follow-up (repeat HIV, STI, adherence)
- **H**BV status (HepB)
- **B**aseline renal function (Cr/eGFR)
- **C**heck STIs (GC/CT/syphilis/HCV)

### **Geriatric Fall Risk: "3 Questions"**
1. Have you **fallen** in the past year?
2. Do you feel **unsteady** when standing/walking?
3. Are you **worried** about falling?
**Any YES** = full fall risk assessment + TUG test

### **Delirium vs Dementia: "ACUTE vs CHRONIC"**
**DELIRIUM** = **A**CUTE  
- **A**cute onset (hours-days)
- **A**ttention impaired
- **A**lteration in consciousness (fluctuates)
- **R**eversible (find cause!)

**DEMENTIA** = **C**HRONIC  
- **C**hronic onset (months-years)
- **C**onsciousness normal (until late)
- **P**rogressive
- **I**rreversible

### **Beers Criteria: "3 B's to AVOID"**
- **B**enzodiazepines (fall risk, confusion)
- **B**enadryl (anticholinergic)
- **B**arbiturates (CNS depression)

### **POLST vs Advance Directive: "Sick vs Healthy"**
- **POLST** = **Sick** patients (serious illness, limited prognosis) = **Medical ORDERS**
- **Advance Directive** = **Healthy** patients (planning ahead) = **Wishes/preferences**

### **Palliative vs Hospice: "Any Time vs End Time"**
- **Palliative** = **ANY** time (alongside curative treatment)
- **Hospice** = **END** time (≤6 months, comfort only)

### **Table 1: Vaginitis at a Glance**

| Feature | Bacterial Vaginosis | Trichomonas | Candida |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|---------|
| **Discharge** | Thin, gray, homogeneous | Frothy, yellow-green | Thick, white, cottage cheese |
| **Odor** | ✅ Fishy (whiff test +) | ✅ Malodorous | ❌ None |

| **Wet Mount** | Clue cells | Motile trichomonads | Budding hyphae/pseudohyphae (KOH) |
| **Symptoms** | Minimal pruritis | Pruritus, dysuria, dyspareunia | Intense pruritus, burning |
| **Special Finding** | — | Strawberry cervix | — |
| **STI?** | ❌ No | ✅ Yes (treat both partners) | ❌ No |
| **Treatment** | Metronidazole 500mg BID × 7d | Metronidazole 2g × 1 (both) | Fluconazole 150mg × 1 |

### **Table 2: STI Ulcer Comparison**

| STI | Organism | Ulcer Type | Pain | Lymph Nodes | Treatment |
|-----|----------|------------|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Syphilis (1°)** | T. pallidum | Single, hard, indurated (chancre) | ❌ Painless | Firm, non-tender | Penicillin G benzathine 2.4M units IM × 1 |
| **Chancroid** | H. ducreyi | Multiple, soft, purulent | ✅ Painful | Tender, may suppurate (bubo) | Azithromycin 1g × 1 or Ceftriaxone 250mg IM |
| **Herpes Simplex** | HSV-1/HSV-2 | Vesicles → shallow ulcers | ✅ Painful | Tender | Acyclovir, valacyclovir |
| **LGV** | C. trachomatis L1-L3 | Small, painless ulcer → heals | ❌ → ✅ | Painful inguinal buboes | Doxycycline 100mg BID × 21d |

### **Table 3: Pregnancy HTN Disorders**

| Condition | Timing | BP | Proteinuria | Features |
|-----------|--------|-----|-------------|----------|
| **Chronic HTN** | Before 20 weeks | ≥140/90 | May or may not | Pre-existing or diagnosed <20wks |
| **Gestational HTN** | After 20 weeks | ≥140/90 | ❌ None | No end-organ damage; watch for preeclampsia |
| **Preeclampsia** | After 20 weeks | ≥140/90 | ✅ ≥300mg/24hr OR Cr ratio ≥0.3 | May have severe features (HELP-T) |
| **Preeclampsia w/ Severe Features** | After 20 weeks | ≥160/110 OR | ✅ OR end-organ | Headache, visual Δ, RUQ pain, platelets <100K, Cr >1.1, LFTs 2×, pulmonary edema |
| **Eclampsia** | After 20 weeks | Preeclampsia + | ✅ | **SEIZURES** |
| **HELLP** | After 20 weeks | Usually | ✅ | **H**emolysis + **E**levated **L**iver + **L**ow **P**latelets |

**Management Pearl:** All preeclampsia → delivery is definitive cure. Severe features or ≥37 weeks → deliver. <37 weeks + mild → close monitoring. Give **magnesium sulfate** for seizure prophylaxis.

### **Table 4: Antepartum Bleeding**

| Condition | Pain | Blood Type | Risk Factors | Key Finding | Management |
|-----------|------|------------|--------------|-------------|------------|
| **Placenta Previa** | ❌ Painless | Bright red | Prior C-section, multiparity, advanced maternal age | Placenta covers cervical os | ⛔ **NO digital exam!** Ultrasound first, C-section |
| **Abruptio Placentae** | ✅ Painful | Dark red/brown | HTN, cocaine, trauma, smoking | Rigid/tender uterus, fetal distress | Emergency delivery, monitor DIC |
| **Vasa Previa** | ❌ Painless | Bright red (fetal blood) | Velamentous cord insertion | Rupture of membranes → bleeding + fetal bradycardia | Emergency C-section |

### **Table 6: Testicular Masses**

| Condition | Age | Pain | Trans-illumination | Special Features | Management |
|-----------|-----|------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Testicular Torsion** | Adolescents/young adults | ✅ Severe, sudden | ❌ No | Absent cremasteric reflex, high-riding testis, horizontal lie | **EMERGENCY** surgery <6hr |
| **Epididymitis** | Young (STI) or older (E.coli) | ✅ Gradual onset | ❌ No | ⊕ Prehn's sign (relief with elevation), fever | Antibiotics: <35yo Ceftriaxone + doxy; >35yo fluoroquinolone |

### **Table 7: HIV PrEP vs PEP**

| Feature | PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis) | PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis) |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **When** | BEFORE potential exposure | WITHIN 72 hours AFTER exposure |
| **Who** | High-risk individuals (MSM, IVDU, serodiscordant partners) | Anyone with known/high-risk exposure |
| **Regimen** | TDF/FTC (Truvada) or TAF/FTC (Descovy) daily; OR cabotegravir IM q2mo | 3-drug ART (e.g., TDF/FTC + raltegravir) × 28 days |
| **Efficacy** | >99% if adherent | ~81% if started within 72hr |
| **Baseline Labs** | HIV (must be negative), Cr/eGFR, HBV, STI screen | HIV, baseline labs, repeat at 4-6wks, 3mo |
| **Follow-up** | q3mo: HIV test, STI screen, adherence check | Follow-up HIV testing at 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months |

| Tool | Purpose | Scoring | Interpretation |
|------|---------|---------|----------------|
| **TUG (Timed Up & Go)** | Fall risk & mobility | Time to rise, walk 10ft, turn, return, sit | >12 sec = ↑ fall risk |

| **MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment)** | Cognitive screening (more sensitive) | 0-30 points | <26 = cognitive impairment |
| **PHQ-9** | Depression | 0-27 points | ≥10 = moderate-severe depression |

### **Table 9: Beers Criteria — High-Risk Meds for Older Adults**

| Drug Class | Examples | Why Avoid | Alternative |
|------------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Anticholinergics** | Diphenhydramine (Benadryl), hydroxyzine, first-gen antihistamines | Confusion, falls, urinary retention, constipation | Cetirizine, loratadine |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Diazepam, alprazolam, lorazepam | Falls, cognitive impairment, delirium | SSRIs, buspirone, CBT |
| **First-gen antihistamines** | Chlorpheniramine, promethazine | Anticholinergic effects | Non-sedating antihistamines |
| **Tricyclic antidepressants** | Amitriptyline, doxepin | Anticholinergic, orthostatic hypotension | SSRIs, SNRIs |

| **Sliding scale insulin alone** | Regular insulin PRN | Hypoglycemia risk without basal | Basal-bolus regimen |

## 🎯 HIGH-YIELD CLINICAL PEARLS

### **Women's Health**

#### **Contraception Quick Reference**
- **Most effective:** IUD, implant (>99%)
- **Less user-dependent:** Depot medroxyprogesterone (DMPA) injection q3mo
- **Requires daily adherence:** OCPs
- **Emergency contraception:** Levonorgestrel (Plan B) up to 72hr; Ulipristal (ella) up to 120hr; Copper IUD up to 5 days (most effective)

#### **Breast Cancer Screening**
- **USPSTF 2024:** Biennial mammogram starting **age 40** (updated from 50)
- **High risk** (BRCA, strong family history): Start earlier, consider MRI

#### **Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB)**
- **Reproductive age:** Pregnancy test FIRST, then r/o structural (fibroids, polyps), ovulatory dysfunction, medications
- **Postmenopausal bleeding:** Endometrial cancer until proven otherwise → endometrial biopsy

### **Obstetrics**

#### **First Trimester**
- **Dating:** Most accurate by first-trimester ultrasound (crown-rump length)
- **Prenatal labs:** CBC, blood type/Rh, antibody screen, rubella, syphilis, HIV, HBsAg, urine culture, GC/CT
- **High-risk screening:** Offer cell-free DNA or quad screen (15-20wks) + NT scan (11-14wks)

#### **Second/Third Trimester**
- **GDM screen:** 24-28 weeks (all patients)
- **Tdap:** 27-36 weeks (every pregnancy)
- **GBS screen:** 35-37 weeks
- **Kick counts:** Start at 28 weeks (normal ≥10 movements in 2 hours)

#### **Postpartum Depression**
- Screen with **Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS)** at postpartum visits
- Score ≥13 → further evaluation
- Can start SSRIs (sertraline safe in breastfeeding)

### **Men's Health**

#### **Erectile Dysfunction**
- **First-line:** PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil) — contraindicated with nitrates
- **Workup:** Check testosterone (AM level), lipids, glucose, thyroid; assess CV risk

#### **Hypogonadism**
- **Symptoms:** ↓ libido, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, ↓ muscle mass
- **Diagnosis:** 2 morning testosterone levels <300 ng/dL
- **Treatment:** Testosterone replacement (contraindicated if planning fertility — use clomiphene instead)

### **LGBTQIA+ Health**

#### **Transgender Hormone Therapy Monitoring**
- **Trans women (MTF on estrogen):** Monitor for VTE, CV risk, prolactin levels; maintain prostate cancer screening
- **Trans men (FTM on testosterone):** Monitor for polycythemia (Hct), lipids, hepatic function; cervical cancer screening if cervix present

#### **Mpox (Monkeypox) 2022 Update**
- Primarily affecting MSM during outbreak
- Presentation: Painful genital/perianal lesions, lymphadenopathy, fever
- Treatment: Supportive; tecovirimat (TPOXX) for severe cases
- Prevention: JYNNEOS vaccine (2 doses)

### **Geriatrics**

#### **Polypharmacy Management**
- **≥5 medications** = increased risk of adverse effects, drug interactions, falls
- **Deprescribing:** Regularly review and discontinue unnecessary medications
- **STOPP/START Criteria:** Screening Tool of Older Persons' Prescriptions / Screening Tool to Alert to Right Treatment

#### **Osteoporosis Screening**
- **USPSTF:** DEXA scan for all women ≥65; younger postmenopausal women if FRAX indicates
- **Men:** Screen if risk factors (steroids, hypogonadism, fragility fracture)
- **Treatment:** Bisphosphonates first-line (alendronate, risedronate); denosumab if intolerant

#### **Urinary Incontinence Types**
- **Stress:** Leakage with cough, sneeze, laugh → pelvic floor PT
- **Urge:** Sudden urge + leakage → bladder training, antimuscarinics (avoid in dementia — anticholinergic)
- **Overflow:** Incomplete bladder emptying → catheterization, treat obstruction (BPH)
- **Functional:** Physical/cognitive barriers → modify environment

#### **Pressure Injury Prevention**
- **Braden Scale:** Risk assessment (6-23; ≤18 = at risk)
- **Prevention:** Repositioning q2h, pressure-reducing surfaces, skin care, nutrition
- **Staging:** I (non-blanchable erythema) → IV (full-thickness tissue loss)

### **Special Populations**

#### **IPV Screening**
- **Tools:** HITS (Hurt, Insult, Threaten, Scream), RADAR
- **Documentation:** Quote patient verbatim, photograph injuries (with consent), assess safety
- **Resources:** National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233), local shelters, safety planning

#### **Substance Use Disorder**
- **Alcohol:** AUDIT-C (screening), CIWA-Ar (withdrawal), thiamine + folate + multivitamin
- **Opioid:** MOUD = Medications for Opioid Use Disorder (buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone)
- **Tobacco:** Counsel ALL patients; first-line = varenicline or bupropion + NRT

#### **Chronic Pain Management**
- **Non-opioid first:** NSAIDs, acetaminophen, gabapentinoids, SNRIs (duloxetine), topicals
- **Non-pharmacologic:** PT, CBT, acupuncture, exercise
- **Opioids:** Last resort; start low, go slow, frequent reassessment, calculate MME, avoid benzos

## 🧩 ACTIVE LEARNING ACTIVITIES

### **Activity 1: Mnemonic Flashcards**
Create physical or digital flashcards:
- **Front:** Mnemonic name (e.g., "HELP-T")
- **Back:** What it stands for + context
- Shuffle daily, test recall

### **Activity 2: Teach-Back Method**
- Pick a topic (e.g., "Delirium vs Dementia")
- Explain it out loud as if teaching a classmate
- Record yourself and listen back
- Note any gaps in understanding

### **Activity 3: Case Creation**
Write your own clinical vignettes:
- **Example:** "A 72yo with baseline dementia presents with acute confusion over 24 hours. Vitals: T 101.8°F. What's your diagnosis and workup?"
- This forces active recall and application

### **Activity 4: Comparison Poster**
Make a visual poster/mind map for ONE comparison table:
- Use colors, arrows, drawings
- Hang it where you'll see it daily (bathroom mirror, desk)
- Great for visual learners

### **Activity 5: Spaced Repetition**
- Review new material today
- Review again in 2 days
- Review again in 1 week
- This builds long-term retention

### **Activity 6: Movement + Mnemonics**
- Assign a physical gesture to each mnemonic
- Example: For "HELP-T," touch your Head, Elevated hands (liver), Low fingers (platelets), Puff cheeks (pulmonary), Thumbs down (thrombocytopenia)
- Movement aids memory encoding (especially for ADHD brains)

## 🎯 TEST-TAKING STRATEGIES

### **Strategy 1: Keyword Recognition**
Learn to spot these words and what they signal:

| Keyword | What It Means |
|---------|---------------|
| **"FIRST-line"** | Most common initial treatment (not most aggressive) |
| **"Most likely"** | Classic presentation, highest probability |
| **"EXCEPT" / "LEAST likely"** | ⚠️ Look for the WRONG answer |
| **"Next step"** | Follow standard algorithm/protocol |
| **"Emergency"** | Immediate action needed (e.g., torsion = surgery now) |
| **"Screening"** | USPSTF guidelines, population-level (not diagnostic) |

### **Strategy 2: Process of Elimination**
1. Cross out obviously wrong answers
2. Look for absolute words ("always," "never") — often wrong in medicine
3. Choose the safest, most conservative option when unsure
4. Trust your gut on the first read

### **Strategy 3: Timing Management**
- 60 questions in 120 minutes = 2 minutes per question
- Spend <1 min on easy questions (bank time)
- Spend up to 3 min on tough questions
- Flag and skip if stuck >2 min, return later
- Leave NO questions blank

### **Strategy 4: ADHD-Specific Tips**
- ✅ Use scratch paper to jot mnemonics at start
- ✅ Read questions ALOUD (quietly) if testing alone
- ✅ Cover answer choices, read stem first, predict answer
- ✅ Take scheduled breaks (use bathroom, stretch)
- ✅ Use fidget tool if allowed (stress ball, ring)
- ✅ Breathe: 4-count in, 4-count hold, 4-count out between questions
