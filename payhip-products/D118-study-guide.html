Evidence-Based Practice

Question 1

Which study design provides the highest level of evidence for clinical decision-making?

  1. [ ] Case-control study
  2. [ ] Cohort study
  3. [✓] Randomized controlled trial
  4. [ ] Cross-sectional study

Explanation: RCTs are the gold standard — randomization controls for confounders and establishes causation.

Question 2

A patient asks why you're using a specific treatment. You cite a systematic review of RCTs. What USPSTF grade would indicate high certainty of substantial benefit?

  1. [ ] Grade B
  2. [✓] Grade A
  3. [ ] Grade C
  4. [ ] Grade D

Explanation: Grade A = high certainty of substantial net benefit. Grade B = high certainty of moderate benefit.

Preventive Screening

Question 1

At what age does the USPSTF recommend starting colorectal cancer screening for average-risk adults?

  1. [ ] 40 years
  2. [✓] 45 years
  3. [ ] 50 years
  4. [ ] 55 years

Explanation: USPSTF updated recommendation in 2021: start at age 45 for average-risk adults.

Question 2

The USPSTF recommends abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) screening with one-time abdominal ultrasound for which group?

  1. [ ] All adults over 65
  2. [✓] Men 65-75 who have ever smoked
  3. [ ] Women 65-75 who have ever smoked
  4. [ ] Men and women 60-75 who have ever smoked

Explanation: One-time ultrasound screening for men 65-75 who have ever smoked (at least 100 cigarettes). Not recommended for women.

Question 3

USPSTF recommends lung cancer screening with low-dose CT annually for adults aged 50-80 who have what smoking history?

  1. [ ] 10 pack-year history, current smoker
  2. [ ] 20 pack-year history, quit within 15 years OR current smoker
  3. [✓] 30 pack-year history, quit within 10 years OR current smoker
  4. [ ] 15 pack-year history, current smoker

Explanation: USPSTF 2021: 50-80 years old, 20 pack-year history, currently smoke OR quit within past 15 years.

Question 4

Which screening is recommended by USPSTF for all adults regardless of risk factors?

  1. [ ] Hepatitis B
  2. [ ] Hepatitis C in adults 18-79
  3. [ ] HIV in adults 15-65
  4. [✓] Both B and C

Explanation: Both Hepatitis C (18-79) and HIV (15-65) are Grade B recommendations for all adults in those age ranges.

Question 5

USPSTF recommends depression screening for which adult population?

  1. [ ] Adults 18+ with risk factors only
  2. [ ] Adults 18+ with staff-assisted depression care support
  3. [✓] All adults 18+
  4. [ ] Adults 40+ only

Explanation: USPSTF Grade B: Screen all adults 18+ for depression, including pregnant and postpartum women.

Question 6

At what interval should a Pap smear alone be done for women aged 21-65?

  1. [ ] Every year
  2. [ ] Every 2 years
  3. [✓] Every 3 years
  4. [ ] Every 5 years

Explanation: Pap smear alone: every 3 years for women 21-65. Pap + HPV co-testing: every 5 years ages 30-65.

Immunizations

Which vaccines are recommended for ALL adults 19 years and older annually?

  1. [✓] Influenza
  2. [ ] Influenza and Tdap
  3. [ ] Influenza and pneumococcal
  4. [ ] Influenza and zoster

Explanation: Influenza is the only vaccine recommended annually for ALL adults. Others have specific age/risk indications.

Question 2

At what age is the Shingrix (zoster) vaccine recommended?

  1. [✓] 50 years and older
  2. [ ] 60 years and older
  3. [ ] 65 years and older
  4. [ ] 55 years and older

Explanation: Shingrix is recommended for adults 50 and older, given in 2 doses 2-6 months apart, even if prior Zostavax.

EENT

Question 1

A patient presents with unilateral eye redness, mucopurulent discharge, lid crusting, and no pain. Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Viral conjunctivitis
  2. [✓] Bacterial conjunctivitis
  3. [ ] Allergic conjunctivitis
  4. [ ] Iritis

Explanation: Bacterial conjunctivitis: mucopurulent/purulent discharge, lid matting, typically unilateral to start. Treat with topical antibiotics.

Question 2

Which finding in a red eye warrants SAME-DAY ophthalmology referral?

  1. [ ] Bilateral itching and watery discharge
  2. [ ] Unilateral purulent discharge
  3. [✓] Decreased visual acuity with eye pain
  4. [ ] Seasonal exacerbation of redness

Explanation: Decreased VA + eye pain = possible iritis, angle-closure glaucoma, or corneal ulcer — same-day referral required.

Question 3

A child presents with otalgia, fever, and TM with loss of landmarks, bulging, and erythema. First-line treatment?

  1. [ ] Amoxicillin-clavulanate 90mg/kg/day
  2. [✓] Amoxicillin 90mg/kg/day
  3. [ ] Azithromycin
  4. [ ] Watchful waiting for 48-72 hours in all children

Explanation: AOM first-line: amoxicillin 90mg/kg/day (high dose) in children. Augmentin if recent antibiotics or conjunctivitis present.

Question 4

Treatment for allergic rhinitis — which is most effective as monotherapy?

  1. [ ] Oral antihistamines
  2. [ ] Intranasal antihistamines
  3. [✓] Intranasal corticosteroids
  4. [ ] Oral decongestants

Explanation: Intranasal corticosteroids (fluticasone, mometasone) are the most effective single therapy for allergic rhinitis.

Question 5

A patient with 10 days of facial pressure, purulent nasal discharge, and maxillary toothache, not improving. Treatment?

  1. [ ] Saline irrigation only
  2. [ ] Amoxicillin 500mg TID x 5-7 days
  3. [✓] Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875mg BID x 5-7 days
  4. [ ] Azithromycin Z-pack

Explanation: Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis: Amoxicillin-clavulanate is preferred. Amoxicillin alone if no recent antibiotics, mild, no risk factors.

Respiratory - Acute

Question 1

A patient has cough, low-grade fever, wheezing after URI. CXR is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Community acquired pneumonia
  2. [✓] Acute bronchitis
  3. [ ] Asthma exacerbation
  4. [ ] Pertussis

Explanation: Acute bronchitis: follows URI, cough 1-3 weeks, wheezing possible, normal CXR. Usually viral — antibiotics NOT indicated.

Question 2

A 65yo diabetic presents with fever, productive cough, RLL consolidation on CXR. PORT/PSI score suggests outpatient treatment. What is first-line treatment?

  1. [ ] Azithromycin alone
  2. [ ] Amoxicillin alone
  3. [✓] Amoxicillin + azithromycin OR respiratory fluoroquinolone
  4. [ ] IV ceftriaxone

Explanation: CAP outpatient with comorbidities: combo beta-lactam + macrolide OR respiratory fluoroquinolone (levofloxacin, moxifloxacin).

Question 3

What CURB-65 score indicates hospital admission is required for pneumonia?

  1. [ ] Score of 1
  2. [✓] Score of 2 or more
  3. [ ] Score of 3 or more
  4. [ ] Score of 4 or more

Explanation: CURB-65: 0-1 = outpatient, 2 = consider admission, 3+ = hospitalize. Each point: Confusion, Urea>19, RR≥30, BP<90/60, age≥65.

GI - Acute

Question 1

A patient presents with RLQ pain that began periumbilically, fever, nausea, and rebound tenderness. McBurney's point is tender. Next step?

  1. [ ] Abdominal X-ray
  2. [✓] CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast
  3. [ ] Ultrasound abdomen
  4. [ ] Start oral antibiotics and reassess

Explanation: Appendicitis workup: CT abdomen/pelvis is gold standard in adults. Ultrasound preferred in children/pregnant women to avoid radiation.

Question 2

Murphy's sign is positive (inspiratory arrest with RUQ palpation). Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Appendicitis
  2. [✓] Acute cholecystitis
  3. [ ] Peptic ulcer disease
  4. [ ] Pancreatitis

Explanation: Murphy's sign = acute cholecystitis. Caused by RUQ palpation during inspiration when inflamed gallbladder descends onto examining hand.

Urinary - Acute

Question 1

An uncomplicated UTI in a non-pregnant woman. First-line treatment?

  1. [ ] Ciprofloxacin 500mg BID x 3 days
  2. [✓] Nitrofurantoin 100mg BID x 5 days
  3. [ ] Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole DS BID x 7 days
  4. [ ] Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875mg BID x 7 days

Explanation: First-line for uncomplicated UTI: nitrofurantoin 5 days or TMP-SMX 3 days (if local resistance <20%). Fluoroquinolones are NOT first-line.

Question 2

A woman with dysuria, flank pain, fever, CVA tenderness, nausea. Urinalysis shows WBCs, bacteria. Diagnosis and outpatient treatment?

  1. [ ] UTI — nitrofurantoin x 5 days
  2. [✓] Pyelonephritis — ciprofloxacin 500mg BID x 7 days
  3. [ ] Pyelonephritis — TMP-SMX DS BID x 14 days
  4. [ ] Pyelonephritis — amoxicillin-clavulanate x 14 days

Explanation: Uncomplicated pyelonephritis outpatient: ciprofloxacin 500mg BID x 7 days OR TMP-SMX x 14 days if susceptible. Fluoro preferred for kidney penetration.

Renal

Question 1

A patient passes a kidney stone. Most common type of nephrolithiasis?

  1. [ ] Struvite
  2. [ ] Uric acid
  3. [✓] Calcium oxalate
  4. [ ] Cystine

Explanation: 80% of kidney stones are calcium oxalate. Struvite = infection stones. Uric acid = gout. Cystine = genetic disorder.

Dermatology

Question 1

Honey-colored crusted lesions on a child's face around the nose/mouth. Most likely diagnosis and treatment?

  1. [ ] Herpes labialis — acyclovir
  2. [✓] Impetigo — mupirocin topical or amoxicillin-clavulanate
  3. [ ] Contact dermatitis — topical steroids
  4. [ ] Tinea faciei — clotrimazole

Explanation: Impetigo: honey-crusted lesions, common in children, S. aureus or Strep. Localized: mupirocin topical. Widespread: oral antibiotics.

Question 2

A patient with well-demarcated, silvery-scaled plaques on elbows and knees. Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Atopic dermatitis
  2. [✓] Psoriasis
  3. [ ] Tinea corporis
  4. [ ] Seborrheic dermatitis

Explanation: Psoriasis: silver-scaled plaques on extensor surfaces (elbows, knees, scalp). Nail pitting is also common.

Question 3

Dermatophyte infection of the nails (thickened, yellowed, crumbly nails). First-line oral treatment?

  1. [ ] Fluconazole
  2. [ ] Itraconazole
  3. [✓] Terbinafine
  4. [ ] Ketoconazole

Explanation: Onychomycosis first-line oral: terbinafine (Lamisil) 250mg daily x 6 weeks (fingernails) or 12 weeks (toenails). Most effective with fewest drug interactions.

Question 4

A patient with intense nighttime itching, linear burrows between fingers, and similar symptoms in household contacts. Diagnosis and treatment?

  1. [ ] Atopic dermatitis — triamcinolone cream
  2. [✓] Scabies — permethrin 5% cream
  3. [ ] Contact dermatitis — hydrocortisone
  4. [ ] Pediculosis — permethrin 1% rinse

Explanation: Scabies: Sarcoptes scabiei mite. Key features: nocturnal itching, burrows, household spread. Treat ALL household members with permethrin 5% from neck down.

Asthma

Question 1

A patient with asthma uses their rescue inhaler more than 2 days/week and wakes up with symptoms 3-4 nights/month. How is this classified?

  1. [ ] Intermittent asthma
  2. [✓] Mild persistent asthma
  3. [ ] Moderate persistent asthma
  4. [ ] Severe persistent asthma

Explanation: Mild persistent: symptoms >2 days/week but not daily, nighttime awakenings 3-4x/month, minor limitation. Step 2 therapy: low-dose ICS.

Question 2

What is the FIRST step in pharmacologic management of persistent asthma?

  1. [ ] Add LABA
  2. [✓] Start low-dose inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)
  3. [ ] Start oral prednisone
  4. [ ] Start montelukast

Explanation: Step 2 (mild persistent): low-dose ICS is the cornerstone. LABAs are NEVER used as monotherapy in asthma — always with ICS.

COPD

Question 1

A 65yo smoker with FEV1/FVC <0.70 and FEV1 55% predicted. Which GOLD classification?

  1. [ ] GOLD 1 — Mild
  2. [✓] GOLD 2 — Moderate
  3. [ ] GOLD 3 — Severe
  4. [ ] GOLD 4 — Very severe

Explanation: GOLD 2 (Moderate): FEV1 50-79% predicted. GOLD 1: ≥80%. GOLD 3: 30-49%. GOLD 4: <30%.

Question 2

Most important intervention shown to slow COPD progression?

  1. [ ] Long-acting bronchodilators
  2. [ ] Inhaled corticosteroids
  3. [✓] Smoking cessation
  4. [ ] Pulmonary rehabilitation

Explanation: Smoking cessation is the ONLY intervention proven to slow decline in FEV1 and alter COPD progression. Everything else manages symptoms.

Pulmonary Embolism

Question 1

A patient with sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, and recent long flight. What is the FIRST diagnostic test?

  1. [ ] V/Q scan
  2. [ ] CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA)
  3. [✓] D-dimer if low pretest probability
  4. [ ] Chest X-ray

Explanation: Use Wells criteria first. If low pretest probability → D-dimer. If elevated → CTPA. CTPA is gold standard but D-dimer can rule out in low-probability patients.

Hypertension

Question 1

A 55yo with BP 145/92 on two separate visits. No other comorbidities. ACC/AHA stage?

  1. [ ] Elevated BP
  2. [✓] Stage 1 HTN
  3. [ ] Stage 2 HTN
  4. [ ] Hypertensive urgency

Explanation: ACC/AHA 2017: Stage 1 HTN = 130-139/80-89. Stage 2 = ≥140/90. First-line lifestyle changes ± medication based on CVD risk.

Question 2

A diabetic patient with HTN and CKD. What is the preferred antihypertensive class?

  1. [ ] Calcium channel blockers
  2. [ ] Beta-blockers
  3. [✓] ACE inhibitor or ARB
  4. [ ] Thiazide diuretics

Explanation: ACE inhibitors or ARBs are preferred in diabetics with HTN and CKD due to nephroprotective effects (reduce proteinuria, slow GFR decline).

Heart Failure

Question 1

A patient with dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, and bilateral crackles. Echo shows EF of 35%. What is the diagnosis?

  1. [ ] HFpEF
  2. [✓] HFrEF
  3. [ ] Diastolic dysfunction only
  4. [ ] Cor pulmonale

Explanation: HFrEF (Heart Failure with reduced EF): EF <40%. HFpEF: EF ≥50%. Treatment differs significantly — ACE/ARB + beta-blocker + aldosterone antagonist for HFrEF.

Question 2

NYHA Class III heart failure is defined as?

  1. [ ] No symptoms
  2. [ ] Symptoms with moderate exertion, slight limitation
  3. [✓] Symptoms with less than ordinary activity, marked limitation
  4. [ ] Symptoms at rest

Explanation: NYHA Class III: marked limitation — comfortable only at rest, symptoms with less than ordinary activity. Class II = slight limitation. Class IV = symptoms at rest.

CAD

Question 1

A patient with stable angina has CCS Class II. What does this mean?

  1. [ ] No angina
  2. [✓] Angina with ordinary activity, slight limitation
  3. [ ] Angina with less than ordinary activity, marked limitation
  4. [ ] Angina at rest

Explanation: CCS Class II: slight limitation — angina with ordinary exertion (walking >2 blocks, climbing >1 flight). Class I = no ordinary activity limitation.

GI - Chronic

Question 1

Most common cause of peptic ulcer disease?

  1. [ ] NSAID use
  2. [✓] H. pylori infection
  3. [ ] Stress
  4. [ ] Alcohol use

Explanation: H. pylori causes ~70% of duodenal ulcers and ~50% of gastric ulcers. NSAIDs are second most common. Test and treat H. pylori first.

Question 2

First-line treatment for H. pylori eradication?

  1. [ ] Omeprazole + amoxicillin
  2. [ ] Clarithromycin triple therapy (PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin)
  3. [✓] Quadruple therapy (PPI + bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline)
  4. [ ] PPI monotherapy

Explanation: Clarithromycin triple therapy 14 days if local clarithromycin resistance <15%. Quadruple therapy preferred in high-resistance areas or prior macrolide exposure.

Question 3

IBS diagnostic criteria (Rome IV): recurrent abdominal pain at least how often, associated with defecation or stool changes?

  1. [ ] Once a month for 3 months
  2. [✓] Once a week for 3 months
  3. [ ] Daily for 1 month
  4. [ ] Twice a week for 6 months

Explanation: Rome IV IBS criteria: recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week for 3 months, associated with 2+ of: related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form.

MSK - Chronic

Question 1

A patient with first MTP joint swelling, severe acute pain, and serum uric acid of 9.2. Synovial fluid shows negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Pseudogout
  2. [ ] Septic arthritis
  3. [✓] Gout
  4. [ ] Rheumatoid arthritis

Explanation: Gout: negatively birefringent needle-shaped monosodium urate crystals. Pseudogout: positively birefringent rhomboid calcium pyrophosphate crystals.

Question 2

First-line treatment for acute gout flare?

  1. [ ] Allopurinol
  2. [✓] Colchicine or NSAIDs
  3. [ ] Prednisone only
  4. [ ] Febuxostat

Explanation: Acute gout: NSAIDs (indomethacin), colchicine, or steroids. Allopurinol and febuxostat are for PREVENTION — do NOT start during an acute attack.

Rheumatic

Question 1

A woman with malar rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, positive ANA and anti-dsDNA antibodies, thrombocytopenia. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Rheumatoid arthritis
  2. [ ] Dermatomyositis
  3. [✓] Systemic lupus erythematosus
  4. [ ] Sjogren syndrome

Explanation: SLE: malar rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, serositis, renal, neuro, hematologic, immunologic (ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm). 4/11 criteria needed.

Question 2

A 70yo woman with bilateral shoulder/hip girdle pain, stiffness worse in the morning, ESR >50. What diagnosis to consider first?

  1. [ ] Fibromyalgia
  2. [✓] Polymyalgia rheumatica
  3. [ ] RA
  4. [ ] Giant cell arteritis

Explanation: PMR: older adults, bilateral proximal muscle pain/stiffness, elevated ESR/CRP, dramatic response to low-dose prednisone. Always rule out giant cell arteritis (temporal arteritis).

Neurology

Question 1

Classic triad of Parkinson's disease?

  1. [ ] Tremor, rigidity, postural instability
  2. [ ] Tremor, chorea, dementia
  3. [ ] Rigidity, spasticity, clonus
  4. [✓] Bradykinesia, tremor, rigidity

Explanation: Parkinson's triad: resting tremor (pill-rolling), rigidity (cogwheel), bradykinesia. Plus postural instability. Dopaminergic neuron loss in substantia nigra.

Question 2

A 40yo woman with relapsing-remitting neurologic symptoms, optic neuritis, Lhermitte's sign, and MRI white matter plaques. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] ALS
  2. [ ] Guillain-Barre syndrome
  3. [✓] Multiple sclerosis
  4. [ ] Myasthenia gravis

Explanation: MS: demyelinating disease, young adults (F>M), relapsing-remitting, optic neuritis, Lhermitte's sign, periventricular white matter lesions on MRI.

Question 3

Migraine with aura vs without aura — what characterizes aura?

  1. [ ] Nausea and vomiting
  2. [ ] Phonophobia
  3. [✓] Fully reversible neurologic symptoms lasting 5-60 min
  4. [ ] Unilateral throbbing pain

Explanation: Migraine aura: fully reversible neurologic symptoms (visual, sensory, speech) developing over ≥5 min, lasting 5-60 min, followed by headache within 60 min.

Diabetes

Question 1

Diagnostic criteria for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (any ONE of these). Which is correct?

  1. [ ] Fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL
  2. [✓] HbA1c ≥6.5%
  3. [ ] 2-hour glucose ≥180 mg/dL on OGTT
  4. [ ] Random glucose ≥180 with symptoms

Explanation: DM diagnosis: FBG ≥126, HbA1c ≥6.5%, 2hr OGTT ≥200, OR random glucose ≥200 with symptoms. Prediabetes: FBG 100-125, HbA1c 5.7-6.4%.

Question 2

First-line medication for Type 2 DM with no contraindications?

  1. [ ] Insulin
  2. [ ] GLP-1 agonist
  3. [✓] Metformin
  4. [ ] SGLT-2 inhibitor

Explanation: Metformin is first-line for T2DM unless contraindicated (eGFR <30, contrast dye, etc.). Add-ons depend on comorbidities — GLP-1 or SGLT-2 if CVD/HF/CKD.

Thyroid

Question 1

A patient with fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, bradycardia, and dry skin. TSH is elevated, free T4 is low. Diagnosis and treatment?

  1. [ ] Hyperthyroidism — methimazole
  2. [✓] Hypothyroidism — levothyroxine
  3. [ ] Subclinical hypothyroidism — monitor only
  4. [ ] Hashimoto's thyroiditis — selenium

Explanation: Primary hypothyroidism: high TSH, low free T4. Most common cause = Hashimoto's (autoimmune). Treatment: levothyroxine, adjust dose to normalize TSH.

Question 2

A patient with heat intolerance, weight loss, tremor, palpitations, diarrhea, exophthalmos, and diffuse goiter. TSH is suppressed. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Toxic multinodular goiter
  2. [✓] Graves' disease
  3. [ ] Thyroid storm
  4. [ ] De Quervain thyroiditis

Explanation: Graves' disease: most common hyperthyroidism, autoimmune (TSH receptor antibodies), exophthalmos (pathognomonic), diffuse goiter, pretibial myxedema.

Mental Health

Question 1

A patient meets criteria for Major Depressive Disorder. First-line pharmacotherapy?

  1. [ ] Bupropion
  2. [ ] Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA)
  3. [✓] SSRI
  4. [ ] Benzodiazepine

Explanation: SSRIs are first-line for MDD due to efficacy and tolerability profile. SNRIs are also acceptable. TCAs and MAOIs are second/third line due to side effect profiles.

Question 2

DSM-5 criteria for MDD requires how many symptoms for at least how many weeks?

  1. [ ] 3 symptoms for 1 week
  2. [✓] 5 symptoms for 2 weeks
  3. [ ] 4 symptoms for 2 weeks
  4. [ ] 5 symptoms for 1 month

Explanation: MDD: 5+ symptoms for ≥2 weeks, including depressed mood OR anhedonia. Symptoms: sleep, interest, guilt, energy, concentration, appetite, psychomotor, suicidality.

Question 3

A patient on an SSRI for 4 weeks reports little improvement. What is the appropriate next step?

  1. [ ] Switch to a TCA immediately
  2. [✓] Increase dose if at submaximal dosing or switch after adequate trial (6-8 weeks)
  3. [ ] Add a benzodiazepine
  4. [ ] Add lithium augmentation

Explanation: Adequate antidepressant trial = 6-8 weeks at therapeutic dose. At 4 weeks with no response: ensure therapeutic dosing. Full trial before switching.

Cardiovascular - Case

Question 1

A 58yo male smoker with DM and HTN presents with 20 min of substernal chest pressure radiating to left arm, diaphoresis, and nausea. BP 155/95, HR 100. EKG shows ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF. What is the most likely diagnosis and immediate management priority?

  1. [ ] NSTEMI — start heparin and schedule cath within 24-72 hrs
  2. [✓] Inferior STEMI — activate cath lab, aspirin 325mg, heparin, call EMS transfer
  3. [ ] Stable angina — nitroglycerine and discharge with stress test
  4. [ ] Unstable angina — admit for monitoring

Explanation: ST elevation in II, III, aVF = inferior STEMI (RCA). Time is muscle — activate cath lab immediately. Aspirin 325mg chewed, heparin, O2 if hypoxic, nitroglycerin (CAUTION in inferior MI — may cause hypotension if RV involved).

Respiratory - Case

Question 1

A 72yo nursing home patient with fever 102, HR 110, RR 24, BP 105/70, O2 sat 88% on room air, and left lower lobe infiltrate on CXR. CURB-65 score = 4. Best next step?

  1. [ ] Discharge with oral levofloxacin
  2. [ ] Admit to general floor with IV antibiotics
  3. [✓] ICU admission given score and hemodynamics
  4. [ ] Start azithromycin and recheck in 48 hours

Explanation: CURB-65 of 4 + hemodynamic instability (hypotension, tachycardia) + O2 sat 88% = severe CAP requiring ICU-level care. IV broad-spectrum antibiotics, respiratory support.

Diabetes - Case

Question 1

A 52yo with T2DM, HTN, and established ASCVD (prior MI) presents for medication management. HbA1c is 8.2%. They are on metformin. What medication should be added given their cardiovascular history?

  1. [ ] Add sulfonylurea (glipizide)
  2. [✓] Add GLP-1 agonist (semaglutide or liraglutide) with proven CV benefit
  3. [ ] Add DPP-4 inhibitor (sitagliptin)
  4. [ ] Add insulin glargine

Explanation: ADA guidelines: T2DM + established ASCVD → add GLP-1 RA (semaglutide, liraglutide) OR SGLT-2 inhibitor with proven CV benefit regardless of HbA1c. Reduces MACE.
