🫁 ABG (Arterial Blood Gas)

TestNormal RangeMeaning
pH7.35-7.45<7.35 = acidosis | >7.45 = alkalosis
PaCO₂35-45 mmHg>45 = respiratory acidosis (hypoventilation) | <35 = respiratory alkalosis (hyperventilation)
PaO₂80-100 mmHg<80 = hypoxemia | <60 = severe hypoxemia
HCO₃22-26 mEq/L<22 = metabolic acidosis | >26 = metabolic alkalosis
O₂ Sat95-100%<90% = hypoxemia, needs supplemental O₂
Base Excess−2 to +2 mEq/L<−2 = metabolic acidosis | >+2 = metabolic alkalosis

ABG Interpretation: ROME — Respiratory Opposite: pH ↑ = PaCO₂ ↓ (alkalosis), pH ↓ = PaCO₂ ↑ (acidosis). Metabolic Equal: pH ↑ = HCO₃ ↑ (alkalosis), pH ↓ = HCO₃ ↓ (acidosis).

❤️ Cardiac Markers

TestNormal Range↑ High Indicates
Troponin I<0.04 ng/mLMI (gold standard — rises 4-6h, peaks 12-24h, lasts 7-14d), myocarditis, PE, renal failure
Troponin T<0.01 ng/mLMI (similar kinetics to troponin I), chronic kidney disease can cause mild elevation
CK-MB0-3 ng/mLMI (rises 3-4h, peaks 12-24h, normalizes 48-72h), myocarditis, cardiac surgery
BNP<100 pg/mLHeart failure (↑ with severity), pulmonary HTN, cor pulmonale, renal failure

ALP44-147 U/LBiliary obstruction, cholestasis, bone disease (Paget's), liver mets
Total Bilirubin0.1-1.2 mg/dLJaundice — pre-hepatic (hemolysis), hepatic (hepatitis, cirrhosis), post-hepatic (obstruction)
Direct Bilirubin0-0.3 mg/dLObstructive jaundice (gallstones, tumor), drug-induced cholestasis
GGT8-61 U/LAlcohol use (very sensitive), biliary disease, drug-induced liver injury. Confirms elevated ALP is hepatic (not bone).
