Development

Question 1

At what age should an infant demonstrate a social smile?

  1. [ ] 1-2 weeks
  2. [✓] 4-6 weeks
  3. [ ] 3-4 months
  4. [ ] 6 months

Explanation: Social smile appears at 4-6 weeks. If absent by 2 months = developmental red flag requiring evaluation.

Question 2

The Denver Developmental Screening Test (ASQ-3) screens which domains?

  1. [ ] Motor only
  2. [ ] Language and social only
  3. [✓] Personal-social, fine motor, language, gross motor
  4. [ ] Cognitive and academic only

Explanation: ASQ-3 (Ages and Stages Questionnaire): screens communication, gross motor, fine motor, problem-solving, and personal-social domains.

Question 3

A 24-month-old cannot say 2-word phrases and has fewer than 50 words. What is the appropriate next step?

  1. [ ] Reassure parents — boys develop more slowly
  2. [✓] Refer for speech-language evaluation and hearing test
  3. [ ] Recheck at 3-year visit
  4. [ ] Screen for autism with M-CHAT only

Explanation: Language red flag: no 2-word phrases by 24 months. Refer for speech eval AND hearing test (hearing loss is common cause of language delay).

Question 4

APGAR scoring is done at what time points after birth?

  1. [ ] 1 and 3 minutes
  2. [✓] 1 and 5 minutes
  3. [ ] 2 and 5 minutes
  4. [ ] 1, 5, and 10 minutes

Explanation: APGAR scored at 1 and 5 minutes. If 5-min score <7, repeat at 10 min. Scores: 7-10 normal, 4-6 moderate concern, 0-3 requires immediate resuscitation.

Question 5

APGAR score components (APGAR mnemonic)?

  1. [ ] Activity, Pulse, Grimace, Appearance, Respiration
  2. [ ] Alertness, Posture, Growth, Activity, Reflexes
  3. [✓] Appearance (color), Pulse, Grimace, Activity, Respiration
  4. [ ] Apnea, Pulse, Glucose, Appearance, Respiration

Explanation: APGAR: Appearance (color), Pulse, Grimace (reflex irritability), Activity (muscle tone), Respiration. Each scored 0-2, max 10.

Question 6

Tanner Stage 2 in girls is characterized by what first change?

  1. [ ] Pubic hair
  2. [ ] Menarche
  3. [✓] Breast budding (thelarche)
  4. [ ] Axillary hair

Explanation: Tanner Stage 2 girls: breast budding is first sign of puberty (thelarche), typically ages 8-13. Pubic hair may appear slightly after. Average menarche at Tanner 3-4.

Question 7

The Barlow and Ortolani maneuvers are used to screen for which condition?

  1. [ ] Tibial torsion
  2. [✓] Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH)
  3. [ ] Legg-Calve-Perthes disease
  4. [ ] Scoliosis

Explanation: Barlow (posterior hip dislocation) and Ortolani (reduction of dislocated hip) maneuvers screen for DDH in newborns. Positive = clunk sensation.

Question 8 — ADHD Screening & Diagnosis

DSM-5 ADHD diagnosis requires symptoms present before what age and in how many settings?

  1. [ ] Age 7 in 1 setting
  2. [✓] Age 12 in 2 or more settings
  3. [ ] Age 10 in 2 or more settings
  4. [ ] Age 7 in any setting

Explanation: DSM-5 ADHD: 6+ inattentive OR 6+ hyperactive symptoms (5+ if age ≥17), present BEFORE age 12, in 2+ settings, causing impairment.

Newborn

Question 1

What is the normal newborn respiratory rate?

  1. [ ] 10-20/min
  2. [ ] 20-30/min
  3. [✓] 30-60/min
  4. [ ] 60-80/min

Explanation: Normal newborn RR: 30-60 breaths/min. HR: 120-160/min. RR >60 = tachypnea, evaluate for respiratory distress.

Question 2

A newborn has central cyanosis at 10 minutes of life. What is the appropriate action?

  1. [ ] Normal — acrocyanosis is expected
  2. [✓] Supplemental oxygen and evaluate for congenital heart disease
  3. [ ] Observe for 30 minutes then reassess
  4. [ ] Apply warming blankets only

Explanation: Acrocyanosis (blue hands/feet) is NORMAL in newborns. Central cyanosis (blue lips/tongue) is ABNORMAL at any time and requires immediate evaluation (CHD, respiratory distress).

Well Child

Question 1

At what ages are routine well child visits recommended in the first year? (AAP schedule)

  1. [✓] 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12 months
  2. [ ] 2, 4, 6, 9, 12 months
  3. [ ] 1, 2, 4, 6, 12 months
  4. [ ] Birth, 1, 3, 6, 9, 12 months

Explanation: AAP Bright Futures schedule: newborn (3-5 days), then 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12 months in first year. Then 15, 18, 24, 30 months, then annually.

Question 2

The HEEADSSS assessment is used for which age group?

  1. [ ] Newborns
  2. [ ] Toddlers 2-5 years
  3. [✓] Adolescents
  4. [ ] School-age children 6-11

Explanation: HEEADSSS: adolescent psychosocial assessment — Home, Education/Employment, Eating, Activities, Drugs, Sexuality, Suicide/depression, Safety. Used ages 11-21.

Immunizations

Question 1

At the 2-month well child visit, which vaccines are given?

  1. [✓] DTaP, Hib, PCV, IPV, HepB, Rotavirus
  2. [ ] MMR, Varicella, HepA, HepB
  3. [ ] DTaP, Hib, PCV, IPV only
  4. [ ] MMR, DTaP, IPV, Varicella

Explanation: 2-month vaccines: DTaP, Hib, PCV13, IPV, HepB (2nd dose), Rotavirus. The 'big 6' at 2, 4, and 6 months.

Question 2

MMR and Varicella vaccines are first given at what age?

  1. [ ] 6 months
  2. [ ] 9 months
  3. [✓] 12-15 months
  4. [ ] 18 months

Explanation: MMR and Varicella: first dose at 12-15 months, second dose at 4-6 years. Cannot give before 12 months due to maternal antibodies.

Infectious Disease

Question 1

Most common cause of childhood infectious illness?

  1. [ ] Bacteria
  2. [ ] Fungi
  3. [✓] Viruses
  4. [ ] Parasites

Explanation: Viruses are the most common cause of childhood infections (URI, otitis media, gastroenteritis, etc.). Most URIs are viral and do NOT require antibiotics.

Question 2

A child with fever, confluent maculopapular rash starting on face spreading downward, cough, coryza, and conjunctivitis. Koplik spots seen on buccal mucosa. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Rubella
  2. [ ] Roseola
  3. [✓] Rubeola (measles)
  4. [ ] Erythema infectiosum

Explanation: Measles (Rubeola): 3 C's (cough, coryza, conjunctivitis) + fever + maculopapular rash head-to-toe + Koplik spots (pathognomonic). Reportable disease.

Question 3

A child with fever for 3-4 days, then fever breaks and a rose-colored rash appears on trunk spreading outward. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Measles
  2. [ ] Rubella
  3. [✓] Roseola (HHV-6)
  4. [ ] Fifth disease

Explanation: Roseola (Exanthem Subitum): HHV-6/7, high fever 3-5 days, fever breaks, rash appears. Classic 'fever first, rash after fever breaks.' Ages 6 months - 2 years.

Question 4

'Slapped cheek' rash followed by lacy reticular rash on extremities in a child. School-age, low-grade fever. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Roseola
  2. [ ] Rubella
  3. [✓] Erythema infectiosum (Fifth disease, Parvovirus B19)
  4. [ ] Scarlet fever

Explanation: Fifth disease: Parvovirus B19, slapped cheek appearance, followed by lacy reticular rash on arms/legs. Most contagious BEFORE rash — no longer contagious once rash appears. Dangerous in sickle cell and pregnancy.

Question 5

A child with sore throat, fever, and a sandpaper-textured rash on trunk sparing the face, with strawberry tongue and circumoral pallor. Culture grows Group A Strep. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Kawasaki disease
  2. [✓] Scarlet fever
  3. [ ] Roseola
  4. [ ] Meningococcemia

Explanation: Scarlet fever: Group A Strep pharyngitis + diffuse sandpaper rash, circumoral pallor, strawberry tongue. Treat with penicillin/amoxicillin x 10 days to prevent rheumatic fever.

Question 6

Lyme disease first-line treatment in children (except <8 years for doxycycline)?

  1. [ ] Azithromycin 5 days
  2. [ ] Doxycycline 100mg BID x 14-21 days (age ≥8)
  3. [✓] Amoxicillin 50mg/kg/day x 14-21 days (age <8)
  4. [ ] Ceftriaxone IV for all cases

Explanation: Lyme disease: age ≥8 = doxycycline x 14-21 days. Age <8 = amoxicillin (avoid doxycycline — dental staining). IV ceftriaxone for disseminated (cardiac, neuro) disease.

Question 7 — Epiglottitis Emergency

A 4yo unvaccinated child presents with high fever, drooling, severe sore throat, and 'tripod positioning.' Refuses to lie down. HR 140, RR 30. On exam, the child appears toxic and you can barely see the throat. What should you do?

  1. [ ] Attempt to visualize throat with tongue depressor
  2. [✓] Do nothing to agitate child — maintain airway, call ENT/anesthesia/EMS immediately
  3. [ ] Give oral amoxicillin and admit to floor
  4. [ ] Perform lateral neck X-ray first

Explanation: Epiglottitis emergency: do NOT agitate child or attempt throat exam. Maintain calm environment, keep child in position of comfort, call ENT and anesthesia stat for airway management. H. influenzae type b (rare with vaccination).

Respiratory - Peds

Question 1

A 2yo with barky cough, inspiratory stridor, low-grade fever, and hoarse voice. Worsens at night. Diagnosis and treatment?

  1. [ ] Epiglottitis — immediate intubation
  2. [✓] Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis) — dexamethasone ± nebulized racemic epinephrine
  3. [ ] Foreign body aspiration — bronchoscopy
  4. [ ] Bacterial tracheitis — IV antibiotics

Explanation: Croup: barky 'seal-like' cough, stridor, worse at night, para-influenza virus. Mild-moderate: dexamethasone 0.6mg/kg. Severe: add nebulized racemic epinephrine. Steeple sign on X-ray.

Question 2

A toddler suddenly develops unilateral wheezing, respiratory distress, and coughing while playing with toys. No fever. What is the priority concern?

  1. [ ] RSV bronchiolitis
  2. [ ] Croup
  3. [✓] Foreign body aspiration
  4. [ ] Asthma exacerbation

Explanation: Sudden unilateral wheezing + no fever in toddler = foreign body aspiration until proven otherwise. Obtain CXR (may show unilateral hyperinflation or air trapping). Requires bronchoscopy.

Question 3

RSV bronchiolitis — what is the mainstay of treatment?

  1. [ ] Albuterol nebulization
  2. [ ] Systemic steroids
  3. [✓] Supportive care — hydration and oxygenation
  4. [ ] Ribavirin antiviral

Explanation: Bronchiolitis (RSV): supportive care is standard — suction, hydration, O2 if needed. Bronchodilators, steroids, and antibiotics are NOT recommended routinely.

Cardiac - Peds

Question 1

Which innocent murmur is described as a vibratory, musical, low-pitched systolic ejection murmur at the LLSB in children 3-8 years?

  1. [ ] Pulmonary ejection murmur
  2. [ ] Venous hum
  3. [✓] Still's murmur
  4. [ ] Supraclavicular bruit

Explanation: Still's murmur (most common innocent murmur): vibratory/musical, grade 1-3, systolic ejection at LLSB, loudest supine, decreases/disappears with standing/Valsalva.

Question 2

An infant with Down syndrome has a harsh pansystolic murmur at LLSB with loud S2 and frequent respiratory infections. Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] ASD
  2. [ ] VSD
  3. [✓] AV canal defect (AVSD)
  4. [ ] Patent ductus arteriosus

Explanation: AV canal defect (endocardial cushion defect): strongly associated with Down syndrome (trisomy 21). Involves ASD + VSD + abnormal AV valves. Requires surgical repair.

Question 3

A cyanotic neonate with a 'boot-shaped heart' on CXR and harsh systolic murmur. Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Transposition of great arteries
  2. [✓] Tetralogy of Fallot
  3. [ ] Total anomalous pulmonary venous return
  4. [ ] Tricuspid atresia

Explanation: Tetralogy of Fallot: VSD + pulmonary stenosis + overriding aorta + RVH. Boot-shaped heart on CXR. Most common cyanotic CHD after first year. Tet spells — knee-chest position.

GI - Peds

Question 1

A 3-week-old male with projectile non-bilious vomiting after every feeding, hungry immediately after vomiting, and an olive-shaped mass in epigastrium. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] GERD
  2. [ ] Intussusception
  3. [✓] Pyloric stenosis
  4. [ ] Hirschsprung disease

Explanation: Pyloric stenosis: non-bilious projectile vomiting at 2-6 weeks (male > female), olive mass, hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis. Ultrasound confirms. Pyloromyotomy is treatment.

Question 2

A 9-month-old with sudden colicky abdominal pain, drawing up knees, 'currant jelly' stools, and sausage-shaped abdominal mass. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Pyloric stenosis
  2. [ ] Appendicitis
  3. [✓] Intussusception
  4. [ ] Meckel's diverticulum

Explanation: Intussusception: telescoping of bowel, peak 6-18 months. Currant jelly stools (bloody mucus), episodic colicky pain, palpable mass. Air enema is diagnostic and therapeutic.

Question 3

A newborn passes no meconium in first 24-48 hours of life. Rectal exam causes explosive release of stool. Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Pyloric stenosis
  2. [✓] Hirschsprung disease
  3. [ ] Meconium ileus
  4. [ ] Intussusception

Explanation: Hirschsprung disease: absence of ganglion cells in colon. Delayed meconium passage >48 hours, explosive decompression on rectal exam, risk of toxic megacolon. Suction rectal biopsy confirms.

MSK - Peds

Question 1

A 6yo limping with insidious onset hip pain, antalgic gait, decreased internal rotation. X-ray shows flattened femoral head. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Developmental dysplasia of hip
  2. [ ] Slipped capital femoral epiphysis
  3. [✓] Legg-Calve-Perthes disease
  4. [ ] Septic arthritis

Explanation: Legg-Calve-Perthes: avascular necrosis of femoral head, ages 4-10, boys > girls. Flattened femoral head on X-ray. Treat with containment therapy.

Question 2

An obese 13yo male with limp and hip/knee pain. X-ray shows displacement of femoral epiphysis. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Legg-Calve-Perthes
  2. [ ] Septic arthritis
  3. [✓] Slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE)
  4. [ ] DDH

Explanation: SCFE: obese adolescent male, hip/groin pain radiating to knee, X-ray shows 'ice cream falling off cone.' Bilateral in 20-40%. Urgent surgical pinning required — non-weightbearing.

Question 3

A 12yo boy with knee pain at the tibial tuberosity during sports, tender swelling at tuberosity. No joint effusion. Diagnosis?

  1. [ ] Septic arthritis
  2. [ ] Patellofemoral pain syndrome
  3. [✓] Osgood-Schlatter disease
  4. [ ] Meniscus tear

Explanation: Osgood-Schlatter: traction apophysitis at tibial tuberosity, active adolescents during growth spurt, worse with activity, tender tibial tuberosity. Treat with activity modification, stretching, ice.

Neurology - Peds

Question 1

A 2yo has a generalized tonic-clonic seizure lasting 2 minutes with temperature of 103.5F. No neurologic deficits. Prior episodes. What is this most likely?

  1. [ ] Epilepsy requiring AED therapy
  2. [ ] Complex febrile seizure
  3. [✓] Simple febrile seizure
  4. [ ] Meningitis — immediate LP

Explanation: Simple febrile seizure: age 6mo-6yr, generalized, <15 min, occurs once in 24hrs with fever. No treatment needed. Complex = >15 min, focal, or recurs within 24 hrs.

Question 2

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) — which screening tool is used at the 18-month and 24-month well child visits?

  1. [ ] ASQ-3
  2. [ ] NICHQ Vanderbilt
  3. [✓] M-CHAT-R/F
  4. [ ] SCARED

Explanation: M-CHAT-R/F (Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers): ASD screening at 16-30 months. AAP recommends at 18 and 24-month well child visits.

Question 3

A mother brings her 18-month-old because 'he doesn't talk much, doesn't wave bye-bye, and stopped saying mama.' He doesn't make eye contact. What is the priority action?

  1. [ ] Reassure — boys talk later
  2. [ ] Refer for speech therapy only
  3. [✓] Immediate referral for autism evaluation (M-CHAT positive) + audiology
  4. [ ] Wait until 2-year visit to reassess

Explanation: Regression of language + lack of eye contact + no pointing/waving = ASD red flags. Loss of previously acquired skills = IMMEDIATE referral (not watchful waiting). M-CHAT, audiology, developmental pediatrics.

Endocrine - Peds

Question 1

A 7yo girl begins developing breast buds. What is the appropriate evaluation?

  1. [ ] Normal — can begin puberty at age 7
  2. [✓] Bone age X-ray and refer to pediatric endocrinology if early
  3. [ ] Reassure — early thelarche is always benign
  4. [ ] Start GnRH agonist immediately

Explanation: Precocious puberty in girls: breast development before age 8 (or pubic hair before 8). Evaluate with bone age, LH, FSH, estradiol. Refer to endocrinology. GnRH agonist used if central PP confirmed.

Question 2

Which condition is most associated with childhood onset Type 1 DM?

  1. [ ] Insulin resistance
  2. [✓] Autoimmune destruction of beta cells
  3. [ ] Obesity
  4. [ ] Mitochondrial dysfunction

Explanation: Type 1 DM: autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells (islets of Langerhans). Anti-GAD, anti-islet cell antibodies. Requires insulin therapy. DKA is common presentation.

Hematology - Peds

Question 1

A 10-month-old exclusively breastfed infant has pallor, fatigue, and irritability. CBC shows microcytic hypochromic anemia. Most likely diagnosis?

  1. [ ] B12 deficiency
  2. [ ] Thalassemia
  3. [✓] Iron deficiency anemia
  4. [ ] Sickle cell disease

Explanation: Iron deficiency anemia: most common anemia in children ages 6mo-3yr. Breast milk is low in iron — start iron supplementation at 4 months for breastfed infants. Microcytic, hypochromic.

Question 2

A child with sickle cell disease presents with sudden severe bone pain in extremities, chest pain, and fever. Priority assessment?

  1. [✓] Acute chest syndrome vs vaso-occlusive crisis
  2. [ ] Septic arthritis
  3. [ ] Pulmonary embolism
  4. [ ] Osteomyelitis only

Explanation: Sickle cell crises: vaso-occlusive (most common — bone pain), acute chest syndrome (chest pain + fever + infiltrate = life-threatening), stroke, splenic sequestration. Fever = sepsis risk — empiric antibiotics.
