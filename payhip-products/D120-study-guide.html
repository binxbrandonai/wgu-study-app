D120: Special Populations and Advanced Primary Care

Study Guide for WGU FNP Program

Source: Leik FNP 4th Edition Intensive Review

Official OA Competency Breakdown (60 questions total)

The D120 Objective Assessment is structured as follows:

  • Women's Health: 22% (~13 questions)
  • Care of the Pregnant Patient: 14% (~8 questions)
  • Men's Health: 8% (~5 questions)
  • Care of the LGBTQIA Community: 11% (~7 questions)
  • Care of the Older Adult: 20% (~12 questions)
  • Care in Special Populations: 15% (~9 questions)
  • Coordination with Specialty Practices and Inpatient Care: 10% (~6 questions)

Study Strategy: Focus your preparation time proportionally on these areas. Women's Health and Care of the Older Adult together make up 42% of the exam.

Women's Health

Question 1

Four principles of practice in women's health primary care?

  1. [✓] Autonomy, Beneficence, Non-maleficence, Justice
  2. [ ] Prevention, Screening, Treatment, Referral
  3. [ ] Holism, Advocacy, Collaboration, Evidence-based care
  4. [ ] Safety, Efficacy, Equity, Access

Explanation: The 4 bioethical principles: Autonomy (patient's right to decide), Beneficence (do good), Non-maleficence (do no harm), Justice (fair distribution of care). Core to all women's health practice.

Women's Health - Gynecology

Question 1

Classic presentation of bacterial vaginosis (BV)?

  1. [ ] Thick white cottage-cheese discharge, pruritus, pH <4.5
  2. [✓] Thin gray/white homogeneous discharge, fishy odor, pH >4.5, positive whiff test
  3. [ ] Frothy yellow-green discharge, pruritus, strawberry cervix
  4. [ ] Mucopurulent cervicitis, no vaginal discharge

Explanation: BV: Gardnerella vaginalis, thin gray discharge, fishy amine odor (whiff test positive), pH >4.5, clue cells on wet mount. Amsel criteria: 3 of 4. Treat: metronidazole 500mg BID x 7 days.

Question 2

Trichomonas vaginitis — characteristic findings and treatment?

  1. [ ] Clue cells, treat with fluconazole
  2. [✓] Frothy green discharge, strawberry cervix, treat with metronidazole for BOTH partners
  3. [ ] Thick white discharge, treat with topical clotrimazole
  4. [ ] Thin discharge, treat with clindamycin

Explanation: Trich (Trichomonas vaginalis): STI — frothy, malodorous, yellow-green discharge, strawberry cervix (punctate hemorrhages), pH >4.5. Treat BOTH partners with metronidazole 2g single dose.

Question 3

Vulvovaginal candidiasis — diagnosis and treatment?

  1. [ ] pH >4.5, treat metronidazole
  2. [ ] Clue cells on wet prep, treat clindamycin
  3. [✓] pH <4.5, budding hyphae/pseudohyphae on wet prep with 10% KOH, treat fluconazole 150mg x1
  4. [ ] STI — treat both partners

Explanation: VVC (Candida): thick white cottage-cheese discharge, pruritus, burning, pH <4.5, hyphae on KOH prep. Uncomplicated: fluconazole 150mg PO x1 OR topical azoles. Complicated: fluconazole x3 doses.

Question 4

Which STI presents with painful genital ulcers with a soft base (soft chancre)?

  1. [ ] Syphilis (primary)
  2. [ ] Herpes simplex
  3. [✓] Chancroid (Haemophilus ducreyi)
  4. [ ] Lymphogranuloma venereum

Explanation: Chancroid: painful soft ulcer + tender inguinal lymphadenopathy (bubo). Caused by H. ducreyi. Syphilis = painless hard chancre. HSV = painful vesicles/ulcers.

Question 5

Primary syphilis presentation and treatment?

  1. [✓] Painless indurated ulcer (chancre), treat with penicillin G benzathine 2.4M units IM x1
  2. [ ] Painful ulcer, treat with azithromycin
  3. [ ] Diffuse rash and condyloma lata, treat with doxycycline
  4. [ ] Gummas, treat with IV penicillin

Explanation: Primary syphilis: painless, indurated chancre at entry site, heals spontaneously in 3-6 weeks. Treatment: penicillin G benzathine 2.4M units IM single dose. Secondary: rash on palms/soles, condyloma lata. Tertiary: gummas, cardiovascular, neuro.

Question 6

PID (pelvic inflammatory disease) — what is the minimum diagnostic criteria?

  1. [ ] Fever + elevated WBC + cervical motion tenderness
  2. [✓] Cervical motion tenderness (CMT) OR uterine tenderness OR adnexal tenderness
  3. [ ] Positive gonorrhea culture
  4. [ ] Positive pregnancy test ruled out + abdominal pain

Explanation: PID minimum criteria (CDC): cervical motion tenderness (CMT), uterine tenderness, OR adnexal tenderness in sexually active female with pelvic pain and no other etiology. Treat empirically.

Women's Health - Breast

Question 1

USPSTF recommendation for breast cancer screening in average-risk women?

  1. [ ] Annual mammogram starting age 40
  2. [ ] Mammogram every 2 years starting age 40
  3. [✓] Mammogram every 2 years ages 50-74, shared decision-making 40-49
  4. [ ] Annual mammogram starting age 50

Explanation: USPSTF 2024 updated: Biennial mammogram recommended for all women starting at age 40 (updated from 50). Shared decision-making for individual risk.

Obstetrics - Antepartum

Question 1

Naegele's rule for calculating estimated due date (EDD)?

  1. [ ] Last menstrual period + 9 months
  2. [✓] First day of LMP + 280 days (or +7 days -3 months +1 year)
  3. [ ] Date of conception + 266 days
  4. [ ] LMP + 10 months

Explanation: Naegele's rule: First day of LMP + 7 days, then subtract 3 months, add 1 year (= +280 days). Example: LMP Jan 1 → EDD Oct 8.

Question 2

Preeclampsia is defined as?

  1. [ ] HTN >150/100 before 20 weeks gestation
  2. [✓] New onset HTN ≥140/90 after 20 weeks + proteinuria OR end-organ damage
  3. [ ] Chronic HTN + proteinuria at any gestation
  4. [ ] Gestational HTN + headache after 37 weeks

Explanation: Preeclampsia: new HTN ≥140/90 after 20 weeks + proteinuria (≥300mg/24hr or protein:creatinine ≥0.3) OR severe features (platelets <100K, Cr >1.1, LFTs 2x normal, pulmonary edema, headache, visual disturbances).

Question 3

Gestational diabetes — universal screening at what weeks?

  1. [ ] 10-14 weeks
  2. [ ] 16-20 weeks
  3. [✓] 24-28 weeks
  4. [ ] 32-36 weeks

Explanation: GDM screening: 1-hour 50g glucose challenge test (GCT) at 24-28 weeks for all pregnant women. If ≥140 mg/dL → 3-hour 100g OGTT for diagnosis.

Question 4

A pregnant woman at 28 weeks has BP 155/102, severe headache, visual disturbances, and RUQ pain. Labs: platelets 90K, AST 3x normal. What is this?

  1. [ ] Preeclampsia without severe features
  2. [✓] HELLP syndrome
  3. [ ] Gestational HTN
  4. [ ] Chronic hypertension

Explanation: HELLP syndrome: Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets. Severe feature of preeclampsia. Requires delivery regardless of gestational age. IV magnesium for seizure prophylaxis.

Question 5

What is the recommended daily folic acid supplementation for women of childbearing age to prevent neural tube defects?

  1. [✓] 0.4mg (400mcg) daily
  2. [ ] 1mg daily
  3. [ ] 4mg daily
  4. [ ] 2mg daily

Explanation: USPSTF: 0.4-0.8mg (400-800mcg) folic acid daily for all women planning/capable of pregnancy, starting 1 month before conception. High-risk (prior NTD): 4mg daily.

Obstetrics - Complications

Question 1

Placenta previa vs abruptio placenta — key differentiating feature?

  1. [ ] Both present with painful bleeding
  2. [✓] Placenta previa = painless bleeding; Abruptio = painful bleeding
  3. [ ] Placenta previa = painful; Abruptio = painless
  4. [ ] Both are painless

Explanation: Placenta previa: painless bright red bleeding (placenta covering cervical os). Abruptio placentae: painful dark bleeding, uterine tenderness/rigidity, maternal-fetal compromise. NO digital exam with previa.

Men's Health

Question 1

Current USPSTF recommendation for PSA-based prostate cancer screening?

  1. [ ] Screen all men starting at age 50
  2. [✓] Screen all men age 55-69 with shared decision-making
  3. [ ] Recommend against screening for all men
  4. [ ] Screen high-risk men (Black, family history) starting at 40

Explanation: USPSTF Grade C: PSA screening in men ages 55-69 — shared decision-making (individual discussion of benefits/harms). Men <55 or ≥70 — recommend against routine screening.

Question 2

BPH symptoms are assessed using what validated tool?

  1. [ ] PHQ-9
  2. [✓] AUA Symptom Score (IPSS)
  3. [ ] CAGE questionnaire
  4. [ ] HAM-A

Explanation: BPH: International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS)/AUA Symptom Score — 7 questions about urinary symptoms. Mild 0-7, Moderate 8-19, Severe 20-35. Guides treatment decisions.

Question 3

First-line medication for BPH symptoms?

  1. [ ] 5-alpha reductase inhibitor (finasteride)
  2. [✓] Alpha-blocker (tamsulosin, terazosin)
  3. [ ] PDE-5 inhibitor (tadalafil)
  4. [ ] Anticholinergics

Explanation: BPH first-line: alpha-blockers (tamsulosin, alfuzosin) — relax smooth muscle, fast symptom relief. 5-ARIs (finasteride) shrink prostate — used for large glands or combination therapy. Tadalafil approved for BPH.

Question 4

Testicular torsion — time window for surgical salvage?

  1. [✓] Within 6 hours = 100% salvage; after 24 hours = near 0%
  2. [ ] Within 2 hours
  3. [ ] Within 12 hours
  4. [ ] Within 48 hours

Explanation: Testicular torsion: UROLOGIC EMERGENCY. <6 hours = ~100% salvage. 6-12 hours = ~50%. >24 hours = ~10%. NEVER delay for imaging if clinical suspicion high — emergency scrotal exploration.

LGBTQIAP+

Question 1

PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) for HIV prevention — current first-line regimen?

  1. [✓] Tenofovir/emtricitabine (Truvada or Descovy) daily
  2. [ ] Single-dose zidovudine post-exposure
  3. [ ] Monthly injectable cabotegravir only
  4. [ ] Doxycycline daily

Explanation: PrEP first-line: tenofovir disoproxil/emtricitabine (Truvada) OR tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine (Descovy) daily oral. Injectable cabotegravir (Apretude) every 2 months is alternative. >99% effective when taken consistently.

Question 2

CDC HIV screening recommendation for adults?

  1. [ ] Test only high-risk individuals
  2. [✓] One-time screening for all adults 15-65
  3. [ ] Annual screening for sexually active adults, more frequently for high-risk
  4. [ ] Test only when symptoms present

Explanation: USPSTF: HIV screening for ALL adults 15-65 (Grade A). High-risk individuals (MSM, IVDU, multiple partners) should be screened at least annually. Pregnant women screened at first prenatal visit.

Question 3

For transgender women (MTF) on feminizing hormone therapy, what health screening is most important to discuss?

  1. [ ] Prostate cancer screening per age guidelines
  2. [ ] Cervical cancer screening (even without cervix)
  3. [✓] Cardiovascular risk from estrogen therapy + VTE risk
  4. [ ] Testosterone level monitoring only

Explanation: MTF on estrogen: increased VTE risk, cardiovascular risk, prolactinoma risk. Prostate cancer screening still applies (prostate still present). No cervical cancer screen if cervix removed.

Geriatrics

Question 1

The Timed Up and Go (TUG) test is used to assess what in older adults?

  1. [ ] Cognitive function
  2. [✓] Fall risk and mobility
  3. [ ] Depression screening
  4. [ ] Nutritional status

Explanation: TUG test: patient rises from chair, walks 10 feet, turns, returns, sits. >12 seconds = increased fall risk. Simple, validated fall risk screen for older adults.

Question 2

Three screening questions for falls in older adults (per geriatric guidelines)?

  1. [✓] Any falls in past year? Fear of falling? Difficulty walking?
  2. [ ] Pain level, medication count, vision problems?
  3. [ ] Weight loss, continence issues, sleep quality?
  4. [ ] Depression screen, cognitive screen, sensory screen?

Explanation: Three fall screening questions: (1) Have you fallen in the past year? (2) Do you feel unsteady when standing/walking? (3) Are you worried about falling? Yes to any = full fall risk assessment.

Question 3

What is the Beers Criteria used for?

  1. [ ] Assessing fall risk in elderly
  2. [✓] Identifying potentially inappropriate medications for older adults (≥65)
  3. [ ] Grading dementia severity
  4. [ ] Predicting 30-day mortality

Explanation: Beers Criteria (AGS): list of potentially inappropriate medications in adults ≥65 — those with increased adverse effects outweighing benefits. Examples: anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, certain NSAIDs, first-gen antihistamines.

Question 4

Delirium vs Dementia — key differentiating feature?

  1. [ ] Delirium is gradual onset; dementia is acute
  2. [✓] Delirium has acute onset with fluctuating course; dementia has gradual onset
  3. [ ] Both have fluctuating consciousness
  4. [ ] Dementia is reversible; delirium is not

Explanation: Delirium: ACUTE onset, fluctuating course, impaired attention, often reversible with treatment of underlying cause. Dementia: gradual/insidious onset, progressive, stable consciousness (until late stages).

Question 5

What is the POLST (MOLST) form used for?

  1. [ ] Advance directive for young healthy adults
  2. [✓] Medical orders for life-sustaining treatment for seriously ill patients
  3. [ ] Standard DNR for all patients
  4. [ ] Consent form for surgery

Explanation: POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment): medical orders for patients with serious illness/advanced age — documents specific decisions about CPR, mechanical ventilation, hospitalization, artificial nutrition. More actionable than advance directive.

Question 6

Difference between palliative care and hospice?

  1. [ ] They are the same thing
  2. [✓] Palliative care = at any stage of illness alongside curative treatment; Hospice = life expectancy ≤6 months, comfort-focused, no curative treatment
  3. [ ] Hospice can continue curative treatment
  4. [ ] Palliative care is only for cancer

Explanation: Palliative care: symptom management at ANY disease stage, alongside curative therapy. Hospice: subset of palliative care for terminal patients (prognosis ≤6 months), comfort-only, curative therapy stopped.

IPV

Question 1

Which screening tool is recommended by the CDC for intimate partner violence (IPV) screening?

  1. [ ] CAGE questionnaire
  2. [✓] RADAR or HITS
  3. [ ] PHQ-9
  4. [ ] AUDIT-C

Explanation: CDC-recommended IPV tools: RADAR (Remember to ask, Ask directly, Document, Assess safety, Review options) or HITS (Hurt, Insult, Threaten, Scream). Universal screening recommended for reproductive-age women.

Pain Management

Question 1

CDC recommendation for initial opioid prescribing for chronic pain?

  1. [ ] Start high dose and titrate down
  2. [✓] Prescribe lowest effective dose, reassess frequently, use non-opioid therapies first
  3. [ ] Prescribe ER/LA opioids for all chronic pain
  4. [ ] No limits on initial prescribing

Explanation: CDC 2022 guideline: use non-opioid therapies first, prescribe lowest effective dose, immediate-release preferred over extended-release initially, reassess risk-benefit frequently. Calculate MME (morphine milligram equivalents).

Substance Abuse

Question 1

First-line medication for opioid use disorder (OUD)?

  1. [ ] Naltrexone only
  2. [ ] Methadone only
  3. [✓] Buprenorphine/naloxone (Suboxone) — preferred outpatient
  4. [ ] Clonidine for withdrawal

Explanation: MOUD (Medications for Opioid Use Disorder): buprenorphine/naloxone (Suboxone) — first-line outpatient, reduces cravings and withdrawal. Methadone — OTP clinic only. Naltrexone (Vivitrol) — after detox, monthly injection.

Travel Health

Question 1

Where should you check for current travel health advisories and vaccination recommendations?

  1. [ ] WHO website
  2. [✓] CDC Travelers' Health website (cdc.gov/travel)
  3. [ ] Local health department only
  4. [ ] State Department website

Explanation: CDC Travelers' Health (cdc.gov/travel): destination-specific vaccine requirements, disease risks, prophylaxis recommendations. Check before every international travel counseling visit.

Question 2

Malaria prophylaxis for travel to chloroquine-resistant areas — preferred agent?

  1. [ ] Chloroquine
  2. [✓] Atovaquone-proguanil (Malarone) or doxycycline or mefloquine
  3. [ ] Primaquine only
  4. [ ] Amoxicillin

Explanation: Chloroquine-resistant malaria: atovaquone-proguanil (Malarone), doxycycline, or mefloquine. Malarone well-tolerated, start 1-2 days before, during, and 7 days after travel.

Scope of Practice

Question 1

The APRN Consensus Model defines NP practice as?

  1. [ ] Dependent on physician supervision in all states
  2. [ ] Full practice authority in all states
  3. [✓] Standardized licensure, accreditation, certification, education (LACE) framework — practice authority varies by state
  4. [ ] Only hospital-based practice

Explanation: APRN Consensus Model (LACE): Licensure, Accreditation, Certification, Education — provides framework for standardized NP practice. Practice authority varies: Full practice (independent), Reduced, Restricted by state.

Care Coordination

Question 1

Patient-Centered Medical Home (PCMH) is characterized by?

  1. [ ] Specialist-led fragmented care
  2. [✓] Comprehensive, coordinated, accessible care with a personal physician leading a team
  3. [ ] Emergency department as primary access point
  4. [ ] Insurance-driven care decisions only

Explanation: PCMH: personal physician leading a team, whole-person orientation, coordinated integrated care, enhanced access, quality/safety focus. Reduces ED visits, hospitalizations, improves chronic disease management.

Obstetrics - Case

Question 1

A 32yo G2P1 at 36 weeks presents with sudden onset painless bright red vaginal bleeding. No contractions. BP 118/72. Fetal heart tones are reassuring. What is the priority action and what should you AVOID?

  1. [ ] Digital cervical exam to check dilation — then decide
  2. [✓] No digital exam — ultrasound to confirm placenta location, NPO, IV access, type & crossmatch, emergency OB consult
  3. [ ] Start Pitocin and prepare for delivery
  4. [ ] Discharge home with pelvic rest instructions

Explanation: Painless antepartum bleeding at 36 wks = placenta previa until proven otherwise. NO digital exam (can cause massive hemorrhage). Bedside ultrasound first. IV access, type & screen, NPO, emergency OB.

Geriatrics - Case

Question 1

An 82yo with dementia is brought in for confusion and agitation. Her daughter notes this started 2 days ago — she was 'back to normal' last week. Vitals: T 101.2, HR 95, O2 sat 93%. What is the most likely diagnosis and first priority?

  1. [ ] Worsening dementia — adjust antipsychotic
  2. [✓] Delirium — find and treat underlying cause (sepsis, UTI, pneumonia likely given fever and low O2)
  3. [ ] Stroke — immediate CT head
  4. [ ] Psychiatric emergency — psychiatric hold

Explanation: ACUTE change in cognition over days in a patient with dementia baseline = delirium (not dementia worsening). Fever + low O2 sat = likely infectious trigger (UTI, pneumonia). Treat the cause. Avoid antipsychotics unless safety risk.

LGBTQIAP+ - Case

Question 1

A 28yo MSM presents requesting PrEP. He has had unprotected receptive anal sex with 3 partners in the past 6 months. He is HIV negative today. What labs do you need BEFORE starting PrEP?

  1. [ ] HIV test only
  2. [✓] HIV test, renal function (Cr/eGFR), HBV status, STI screening (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis), HCV
  3. [ ] HIV and CBC only
  4. [ ] No labs needed — start PrEP immediately

Explanation: Before PrEP: HIV test (must be negative), renal function (tenofovir is nephrotoxic — check eGFR), HBV status (FTC active against HBV — stopping can cause flare), STI panel. Monitor every 3 months while on PrEP.
