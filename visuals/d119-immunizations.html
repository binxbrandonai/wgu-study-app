💉 D119: Immunization Schedule

Pediatric Vaccine Timeline & Clinical Pearls

🎯 Mnemonic: "2-4-6-12-15-18-4-11"

2, 4, 6 months: The "big batch" visits (DTaP, IPV, Hib, PCV13, Rotavirus)

12-15 months: MMR, Varicella, Hep A series begins

18 months: DTaP #4, Hep A #2

4-6 years: School entry boosters

11-12 years: Tdap, HPV series, Meningococcal

🚫 Contraindications

ALL VACCINES:

  • Severe allergic reaction to prior dose or component
  • Moderate-severe acute illness (defer until recovered)

LIVE VACCINES (MMR, Varicella, Rotavirus, LAIV):

  • Pregnancy
  • Immunocompromised (HIV with CD4 <15%, chemotherapy, high-dose steroids >2 weeks)
  • Recent immunoglobulin (defer MMR/Varicella 3-11 months depending on dose)

✅ NOT Contraindications (Safe to Vaccinate):

  • Mild illness (URI, diarrhea) without fever
  • Antibiotic use
  • Prematurity (use chronological age, give full dose)
  • Breastfeeding
  • Egg allergy (MMR safe, Flu vaccine safe)
  • Family history of adverse reaction
  • Recent exposure to infectious disease
Catch-Up Schedules: NEVER restart a vaccine series — just continue where you left off. Most vaccines: 4 weeks minimum between doses. MMR/Varicella: 3 months if catch-up needed.
Egg Allergy Myth: MMR is grown in chicken embryo fibroblasts (NOT egg protein). Influenza vaccine contains minimal egg protein. Both are SAFE in egg allergy patients.
Prematurity: Use chronological age (not corrected age) for vaccine schedule. Give FULL dose — do not reduce dose for low birth weight.
🔬 High-Yield Vaccine Facts
Vaccine Type Key Facts
Rotavirus Live (oral)
  • First dose: 6 weeks to 14 weeks 6 days
  • Last dose: by 8 months
  • Contraindicated: severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)
MMR Live (injectable)
  • Most common side effect: fever 7-12 days post-vaccine
  • Can cause transient thrombocytopenia (rare)
  • NOT linked to autism (thoroughly debunked)
Varicella Live (injectable)
  • Post-exposure prophylaxis: within 3-5 days of exposure
  • Can cause mild varicella-like rash at injection site
  • 2-dose series: 12-15 months, then 4-6 years
HPV Inactivated
  • 9-valent (Gardasil 9): covers 90% of cervical cancers
  • 2-dose series if started <15 yo (0, 6-12 months)
  • 3-dose series if started ≥15 yo or immunocompromised
  • Ideally given before sexual debut
Meningococcal Inactivated
  • MenACWY: conjugate (ages 11-12, booster at 16)
  • MenB: separate vaccine for high-risk or college students
  • Increased risk: college dorms, military recruits, complement deficiency
Influenza Inactivated OR Live (LAIV nasal)
  • Annual starting at 6 months
  • First-time recipients <9 yo: 2 doses 4 weeks apart
  • LAIV contraindicated: <2 yo, asthma/wheezing, immunocompromised, pregnant
  • Safe in egg allergy
Immunocompromised Patients: NO live vaccines (MMR, varicella, rotavirus, LAIV). Inactivated vaccines are SAFE and recommended. Give PCV13 and PPSV23 for pneumococcal coverage.
