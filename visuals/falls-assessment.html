Falls Risk Factors

🧍 Intrinsic (Patient) Factors

  • Age: Risk ↑ with age (>80 highest risk)
  • History of falls: Strongest predictor of future falls
  • Gait/balance impairment: Muscle weakness, poor balance
  • Vision problems: Cataracts, macular degeneration, glaucoma
  • Orthostatic hypotension: ↓ BP on standing (drop >20 systolic or >10 diastolic)
  • Chronic conditions: Stroke, Parkinson's, arthritis, dementia, diabetes (neuropathy)
  • Cognitive impairment: Dementia, delirium
  • Urinary incontinence/frequency: Rushing to bathroom
  • Fear of falling: Leads to activity restriction → deconditioning
  • Foot problems: Pain, deformities, poor footwear

🏠 Extrinsic (Environmental) Factors

  • Home hazards: Clutter, throw rugs, cords
  • Poor lighting: Dim hallways, stairs
  • Bathroom hazards: Wet floors, no grab bars
  • Stairs: Without railings, uneven steps
  • Slippery surfaces: Wet floors, ice
  • Improper footwear: Slippers, loose shoes, high heels
  • Pets: Tripping hazard
  • Assistive device issues: Improper use or maintenance

💊 High-Risk Medications

  • Sedatives/hypnotics: Benzodiazepines, zolpidem
  • Antipsychotics: All types
  • Antidepressants: SSRIs, TCAs (anticholinergic)
  • Opioids: Any dose
  • Anticholinergics: Antihistamines, bladder antimuscarinics
  • Antihypertensives: Especially alpha-blockers
  • Anticonvulsants: Sedating effects
  • Polypharmacy: ≥4 medications ↑ fall risk

Risk ↑ with each additional medication

Falls Assessment Flowchart

STEP 1: Screen All Older Adults

Ask 3 questions at every visit (age 65+):

  1. Have you fallen in the past year?
  2. Do you feel unsteady when standing or walking?
  3. Are you worried about falling?

Action:

  • NO to all 3: Low risk → Provide fall prevention education, reassess annually
  • YES to any: Proceed to comprehensive fall assessment (Step 2)
STEP 2: Comprehensive Fall Risk Assessment

Perform detailed multifactorial assessment:

Assessment Component What to Evaluate
Fall History Number, location, circumstances, injuries, witnesses, symptoms before fall
Medications Complete list, high-risk meds, recent changes, polypharmacy
Vision Visual acuity, cataracts, glasses current, ophthalmology referral if needed
Cardiovascular Orthostatic BP (lying, sitting, standing at 1 & 3 min), arrhythmias, cardiac exam
Neurological Cognitive screen (MMSE, MoCA), sensory (proprioception, neuropathy), Parkinson's signs
Musculoskeletal Muscle strength, joint range of motion, arthritis, foot problems
Functional ADLs, IADLs, assistive devices, activity level
Environmental Home safety assessment (or referral for one)
STEP 3: Perform Gait & Balance Testing

Functional Assessment Tools

Timed Up and Go (TUG)

Procedure:

  1. Patient starts seated in chair with armrests
  2. Stand up, walk 10 feet (3 meters)
  3. Turn around, walk back, sit down
  4. Time the entire sequence

Interpretation:

  • <10 seconds: Normal
  • 10-14 seconds: Low fall risk
  • >14 seconds: High fall risk
  • >20 seconds: Very high risk, significant mobility impairment

30-Second Chair Stand

Procedure:

  1. Patient starts seated, arms crossed over chest
  2. Stand up and sit down as many times as possible in 30 seconds
  3. Count number of stands

Interpretation:

  • Men 60-69: <14 stands = below normal
  • Women 60-69: <12 stands = below normal
  • Lower = ↑ fall risk

Berg Balance Scale

Description: 14-item objective measure of static and dynamic balance

Tasks include:

  • Sitting/standing balance
  • Transfers
  • Reaching, bending
  • Turning, standing on one foot

Scoring:

  • 0-4 points per item (max 56)
  • <45: High fall risk
  • 45-50: Moderate risk
  • >50: Low risk

Functional Reach Test

Procedure:

  1. Patient stands arm extended forward at 90°
  2. Reach forward as far as possible without stepping
  3. Measure distance reached

Interpretation:

  • >10 inches (25 cm): Low risk
  • 6-10 inches: Moderate risk
  • <6 inches: High fall risk
STEP 4: Identify Specific Risk Factors

Based on assessment, identify modifiable risk factors:

  • ✅ High-risk medications
  • ✅ Gait/balance impairment
  • ✅ Muscle weakness
  • ✅ Orthostatic hypotension
  • ✅ Visual impairment
  • ✅ Environmental hazards
  • ✅ Footwear issues
  • ✅ Vitamin D deficiency
  • ✅ Cognitive impairment
  • ✅ Postural instability
STEP 5: Implement Multifactorial Interventions

Tailor interventions to individual risk factors - multifactorial approach is most effective

Evidence-Based Interventions

💊 Medication Management

  • Medication review: Identify high-risk medications
  • Deprescribe: Taper/discontinue benzodiazepines, sedatives, anticholinergics
  • Minimize polypharmacy: Target <4 medications if possible
  • Review timing: Avoid doses before bedtime/nighttime bathroom trips
  • Treat orthostatic hypotension: Adjust BP meds, hydration, compression stockings

💪 Exercise Programs

  • Most effective intervention! Reduces falls by 20-30%
  • Balance training: Tai Chi (evidence-based), yoga, standing exercises
  • Strength training: Lower extremity focus, resistance exercises
  • Gait training: Physical therapy referral
  • Duration: 2-3 sessions/week for ≥3 months
  • Group classes: Improve compliance, social benefits

🏠 Home Safety Modifications

  • Lighting: Bright, night lights in hallways/bathrooms
  • Remove hazards: Throw rugs, clutter, cords, low furniture
  • Bathroom: Grab bars, non-slip mats, raised toilet seat, shower chair
  • Stairs: Handrails on both sides, non-slip treads, good lighting
  • Flooring: Secure carpets, repair uneven surfaces
  • OT referral: For comprehensive home assessment

👁️ Vision Correction

  • Annual eye exams: Update prescriptions
  • Cataract surgery: If indicated - reduces fall risk
  • Avoid multifocal glasses: For walking/stairs (bifocals ↑ falls)
  • Treat glaucoma/AMD: Preserve remaining vision
  • Proper lighting: Compensate for vision changes

🦶 Footwear & Foot Care

  • Proper footwear: Low heel, non-slip soles, good support
  • Avoid: Slippers, loose shoes, high heels, shoes with slick soles
  • Podiatry referral: For foot pain, deformities, nail care
  • Orthotics: If indicated for stability
  • Treat foot problems: Corns, calluses, bunions

🩺 Medical Management

  • Vitamin D: 800-1000 IU/day (reduces falls in deficient patients)
  • Calcium: 1200 mg/day for bone health
  • Treat chronic conditions: Parkinson's, arthritis pain, neuropathy
  • Manage orthostatic hypotension: Hydration, salt, compression, medication adjustment
  • Cardiac evaluation: If syncope/pre-syncope

🧠 Cognitive & Behavioral

  • Cognitive screening: Treat reversible causes
  • Address fear of falling: Reassurance, graduated activity
  • Education: Fall risk awareness, prevention strategies
  • Avoid activity restriction: Deconditioning ↑ fall risk
  • Toileting schedule: Reduce urgency-related falls

🛡️ Assistive Devices & Technology

  • Proper assistive device: Cane, walker if indicated (PT assessment)
  • Device training: Correct use, maintenance
  • Hip protectors: For very high-risk patients (limited evidence)
  • Personal alarm: Medical alert system for fall detection
  • Avoid restraints: ↑ injury risk, loss of independence

🚨 Post-Fall Assessment (If Fall Occurred)

IMMEDIATE assessment needed - don't just assume "they're OK"

  • Injuries: Fractures (hip, wrist, spine), head injury, lacerations
  • Vital signs: BP (orthostatic), HR (arrhythmia?)
  • Neurological exam: Mental status, focal deficits (stroke?)
  • Circumstances: Where, when, what happened, witnessed?
  • Symptoms before fall: Dizziness, chest pain, palpitations, confusion
  • Long lie? Time on ground (rhabdomyolysis, dehydration, pressure ulcers)
  • Labs if indicated: CBC, BMP, glucose, ECG, cardiac enzymes
  • Imaging: X-rays for suspected fractures, head CT if head injury/anticoagulated

⚠️ Hip fractures are often missed initially - low threshold for imaging if hip/groin pain after fall

💡 Clinical Pearls for D120:

  • Screen ALL older adults annually - falls are not a normal part of aging
  • Multifactorial interventions work best - address multiple risk factors simultaneously
  • Exercise is #1 evidence-based intervention - specifically balance training (Tai Chi has best evidence)
  • Vitamin D supplementation reduces falls - especially in deficient patients
  • One fall = high risk for another - history of falls strongest predictor
  • Check orthostatic BP properly: Lying → standing at 1 min and 3 min (delayed orthostatic hypotension common)
  • Medication review is critical - polypharmacy and psychotropics are major modifiable risk factors
  • Don't restrict activity - deconditioning increases fall risk; keep patients active with safety measures
  • Home assessment by OT - most cost-effective for high-risk patients
  • Hip fractures have high mortality - 20-30% die within 1 year, many lose independence
  • Educate caregivers - they play key role in environmental modifications and supervision
  • TUG >14 seconds = high risk - simple, quick screening tool for every visit
