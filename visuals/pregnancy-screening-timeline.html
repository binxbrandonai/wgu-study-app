🤰 Prenatal Screening Timeline 🤰

Comprehensive Guide for D120 - From Preconception to Postpartum

Preconception Care (Before Pregnancy)

Optimize maternal health BEFORE conception to improve outcomes

📋 Medical History & Screening

  • Complete medical/surgical history
  • Family history (genetic conditions)
  • Medication review (teratogenic meds)
  • Immunization status
  • Mental health screening
  • Substance use assessment

🩺 Labs & Tests

  • CBC: Screen for anemia
  • Blood type & Rh: Identify Rh negativity
  • Rubella immunity: Vaccinate if non-immune (wait 1 month to conceive)
  • Varicella immunity: Vaccinate if non-immune
  • TSH: Screen thyroid function
  • STI screening: HIV, syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia
  • Hepatitis B & C

💊 Supplementation & Lifestyle

  • Folic acid 400-800 mcg/day (start 1 month before conception)
    • 4 mg/day if history of NTD
  • Optimize chronic conditions (diabetes, HTN, thyroid)
  • Achieve healthy weight (BMI 18.5-24.9)
  • Smoking cessation
  • Avoid alcohol
  • Genetic counseling if indicated

Trimester-by-Trimester Screening

First Trimester
Weeks 0-13

Initial Prenatal Visit Week 6-10

📝 History & Physical

  • Confirm pregnancy (urine/serum βhCG)
  • Calculate estimated due date (EDD)
  • Complete medical/OB/surgical history
  • Medication review
  • Complete physical exam + pelvic exam
  • Baseline vital signs, weight, BMI

🔬 Initial Labs (Comprehensive)

  • CBC: Baseline, screen for anemia
  • Blood type & Rh with antibody screen
  • Rubella IgG: Immunity status
  • Syphilis (RPR or VDRL)
  • Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)
  • HIV (offer to all)
  • Urinalysis & urine culture
  • Pap smear (if due per guidelines)

🧬 Additional Labs (Risk-Based)

  • Varicella IgG: If no history of chickenpox/vaccine
  • Hepatitis C: Universal screening now recommended (ACOG 2021)
  • Gonorrhea/Chlamydia: Age <25 or high risk
  • TSH: If thyroid disorder history or symptoms
  • Hemoglobin electrophoresis: If at risk for sickle cell/thalassemia
  • Tuberculin skin test: If high-risk

🔊 Dating Ultrasound

Timing: Week 8-13 (optimal)

Purpose:

  • Confirm intrauterine pregnancy
  • Establish/confirm gestational age (most accurate)
  • Determine number of fetuses
  • Assess fetal viability (cardiac activity)
  • Screen for major anomalies

💬 Counseling & Education

  • Nutrition counseling
  • Weight gain goals (based on BMI)
  • Folic acid supplementation
  • Prenatal vitamins
  • Activity/exercise recommendations
  • Warning signs (bleeding, cramping)
  • Genetic screening options
  • Avoid teratogens (alcohol, tobacco, drugs)

🧬 Genetic Screening Options

Cell-Free DNA (cfDNA/NIPT):

  • Timing: Week 10+
  • Screens: Trisomy 21, 18, 13, sex chromosome abnormalities
  • High sensitivity/specificity
  • Non-invasive (maternal blood)

First Trimester Combined Screen:

  • Timing: Week 11-13
  • NT (nuchal translucency) ultrasound
  • Maternal serum: PAPP-A, βhCG
  • Screens for trisomy 21, 18

⚠️ First Trimester Red Flags

  • Vaginal bleeding (rule out miscarriage, ectopic)
  • Severe abdominal pain (ectopic pregnancy)
  • Hyperemesis gravidarum (severe N/V, dehydration)
  • Abnormal βhCG levels
Second Trimester
Weeks 14-27

Routine Visits Every 4 weeks

📋 At Each Visit

  • Weight, blood pressure
  • Urine dipstick (protein, glucose)
  • Fundal height measurement (starting ~20 weeks)
  • Fetal heart tones (Doppler)
  • Assess fetal movement (after quickening ~18-20 weeks)
  • Review symptoms, concerns

🧬 Quad Screen (if not done cfDNA)

Timing: Week 15-20 (optimal 16-18)

Measures 4 markers:

  • AFP (alpha-fetoprotein)
  • βhCG
  • Unconjugated estriol
  • Inhibin A

Screens for:

  • Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome)
  • Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome)
  • Neural tube defects (NTDs)

🔊 Anatomy Ultrasound

Timing: Week 18-22 (typically week 20)

Purpose:

  • Detailed fetal anatomy survey
  • Screen for structural anomalies
  • Assess placental location
  • Amniotic fluid volume
  • Cervical length (if indicated)
  • Fetal biometry, growth
  • Sex determination (if desired)

🔬 Glucose Screening

Timing: Week 24-28

1-hour Glucose Challenge Test (GCT):

  • 50g glucose load, check glucose at 1 hour
  • No fasting required
  • If ≥140 mg/dL: Proceed to 3-hour GTT

3-hour Glucose Tolerance Test (if abnormal GCT):

  • 100g glucose load after overnight fast
  • Check glucose: fasting, 1h, 2h, 3h
  • 2+ abnormal values = gestational diabetes

🩸 Repeat/Additional Labs

Week 24-28:

  • CBC: Screen for anemia (repeat)
  • Antibody screen: If Rh negative (repeat)

If Rh negative:

  • RhoGAM at 28 weeks (if antibody screen negative)

💬 Counseling Topics

  • Fetal movement monitoring
  • Preterm labor warning signs
  • Breastfeeding education
  • Childbirth classes
  • Labor/delivery preferences (birth plan)
  • Infant car seat, safe sleep

⚠️ Second Trimester Red Flags

  • Vaginal bleeding (placenta previa, abruption)
  • Preterm labor signs: contractions, pelvic pressure, fluid leakage
  • Decreased fetal movement
  • Severe headache, vision changes (preeclampsia)
  • Rapid weight gain, edema
Third Trimester
Weeks 28-40+

Routine Visits Every 2 weeks (28-36), Weekly (36+)

📋 At Each Visit

  • Weight, blood pressure (watch for preeclampsia)
  • Urine dipstick (protein)
  • Fundal height measurement
  • Fetal heart rate
  • Fetal position (Leopold's maneuvers)
  • Assess fetal movement
  • Assess for contractions, labor signs
  • Cervical exam (if indicated, usually >36 weeks)

🔬 Labs - Week 28

  • CBC: Screen for anemia (3rd check)
  • Glucose screening (if not done week 24-28)
  • Antibody screen (Rh negative patients)
  • RhoGAM administration (if Rh negative, antibody negative)

🦠 GBS Screening

Timing: Week 35-37

Group B Streptococcus (GBS) culture:

  • Vaginal-rectal swab
  • If positive → Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis during labor
  • If negative → No treatment needed
  • If unknown/not done → Treat based on risk factors

🩺 Repeat STI Screening (High-Risk)

Week 36:

  • HIV: Repeat in 3rd trimester (high-risk or opt-out states)
  • Syphilis: Repeat if high-risk
  • Gonorrhea/Chlamydia: Age <25 or high-risk
  • Hepatitis C: If risk factors developed

🔊 Ultrasounds (Indicated)

Not routine, but may be done for:

  • Fetal growth assessment (IUGR concern)
  • Amniotic fluid volume (oligohydramnios/polyhydramnios)
  • Placental location (if previa earlier)
  • Fetal presentation (breech at 36+ weeks)
  • Biophysical profile (BPP) if post-dates or high-risk

📊 Antenatal Testing (High-Risk)

Non-Stress Test (NST):

  • Monitor fetal heart rate response to movement
  • Reactive (normal): 2+ accelerations in 20 min
  • Start week 32+ if high-risk

Biophysical Profile (BPP):

  • NST + ultrasound (movement, tone, breathing, fluid)
  • Score 0-10 (8-10 = normal)

Indications for testing:

  • Post-dates (≥41 weeks)
  • Gestational diabetes, preeclampsia
  • IUGR, decreased fetal movement
  • Maternal conditions (HTN, lupus, renal disease)

💬 Late Pregnancy Counseling

  • Labor signs (contractions, water breaking, bloody show)
  • When to go to hospital (5-1-1 rule)
  • Pain management options
  • C-section indications
  • Postpartum care, recovery
  • Newborn care basics
  • Postpartum depression screening plan
  • Contraception plans

💉 Immunizations

  • Tdap: Week 27-36 each pregnancy (protect infant from pertussis)
  • Influenza vaccine: Any trimester during flu season
  • COVID-19: Recommended any trimester

⚠️ Third Trimester Emergency Signs

  • Preeclampsia: Severe headache, vision changes, RUQ pain, BP ≥140/90
  • Placental abruption: Vaginal bleeding + abdominal pain + contractions
  • Preterm labor: Regular contractions <37 weeks, cervical change
  • Decreased fetal movement: <10 movements in 2 hours
  • Rupture of membranes: Fluid leakage (especially if <37 weeks)

Post-Dates Management Week 40-42

📅 If Pregnancy Continues Past Due Date

  • Week 40-41: Twice-weekly NSTs or BPPs
  • Week 41: Offer induction (discuss risks/benefits)
  • Week 42: Strongly recommend induction (↑ stillbirth risk)
  • Confirm dating with early ultrasound
  • Assess cervical favorability (Bishop score)

Postpartum Care

Immediate Postpartum (Hospital) 0-3 days

👶 Newborn Screening

  • Newborn metabolic screen (heel stick) 24-48 hours
  • Hearing screen
  • Pulse oximetry (CCHD screening)
  • Vitamin K injection
  • Erythromycin eye ointment
  • Hepatitis B vaccine (1st dose)

🩸 Maternal Labs/Assessments

  • Monitor bleeding, vital signs
  • Check hemoglobin if significant blood loss
  • If Rh negative + baby Rh positive: RhoGAM within 72 hours
  • Assess perineal/C-section incision
  • Breastfeeding support

💬 Education & Counseling

  • Warning signs (hemorrhage, infection, DVT)
  • Breastfeeding techniques
  • Newborn care, safe sleep
  • Contraception counseling
  • Postpartum depression education

🩺 Special Situations

  • GBS positive: Ensure adequate intrapartum antibiotics given
  • Diabetes: Check glucose postpartum, plan for follow-up
  • Hypertension: Monitor BP, assess for postpartum preeclampsia
  • HIV positive: No breastfeeding, infant prophylaxis

Postpartum Visit Week 6 (earlier if complications)

📋 Assessment

  • Physical exam, vital signs
  • Pelvic exam (healing assessment)
  • Breastfeeding assessment/support
  • Return of menses, lochia
  • Mood screening (Edinburgh PPD scale)
  • Sleep, energy levels

🔬 Labs & Follow-Up

  • If GDM: 75g OGTT at 6-12 weeks postpartum
  • If hypertension: Check BP, may need continued treatment
  • CBC if anemic: Assess recovery
  • TSH if thyroid disorder

💬 Counseling

  • Contraception: Initiate method if desired
  • Interpregnancy interval (18-24 months optimal)
  • Return to exercise, activity
  • Sexual activity resumption
  • Weight loss goals
  • Mental health support

📅 Future Health

  • Return to routine preventive care
  • Pap smear (if due)
  • Mammogram (if age-appropriate)
  • Chronic disease management

Quick Reference: Screening Timeline

Timing Screening/Test Purpose
Preconception Folic acid, immunizations, optimize health Prevent NTDs, ensure immunity, optimize chronic conditions
First visit (6-10 weeks) CBC, blood type/Rh, rubella, syphilis, HIV, HBsAg, HCV, UA/culture, dating ultrasound Baseline labs, confirm pregnancy, establish EDD
10-13 weeks Cell-free DNA (cfDNA/NIPT) OR First trimester screen (NT + PAPP-A/βhCG) Screen for aneuploidy (trisomy 21, 18, 13)
15-20 weeks Quad screen (if not cfDNA) Screen for aneuploidy, NTDs
18-22 weeks Anatomy ultrasound Detailed fetal anatomy, screen for anomalies
24-28 weeks Glucose screening (GCT), CBC, antibody screen, RhoGAM (if Rh neg) Screen for gestational diabetes, anemia; prevent Rh alloimmunization
27-36 weeks Tdap vaccine Protect infant from pertussis
28 weeks CBC (3rd check), antibody screen, RhoGAM Monitor anemia, Rh status
35-37 weeks GBS culture (vaginal-rectal swab) Determine need for intrapartum antibiotics
36 weeks Repeat HIV, syphilis, GC/CT (if high-risk) Identify late infections
36+ weeks Weekly visits, cervical checks (if indicated), fetal monitoring (if high-risk) Monitor for labor, assess fetal well-being
40-42 weeks NST/BPP, offer/recommend induction Prevent stillbirth in post-dates pregnancy
6 weeks postpartum Postpartum visit, mood screening, contraception, OGTT (if GDM) Assess recovery, screen for PPD, future pregnancy planning

💡 Clinical Pearls for D120:

  • Dating ultrasound most accurate in 1st trimester (within 5-7 days) - use for EDD if discrepancy >7 days from LMP
  • Folic acid 400-800 mcg daily starting 1 month before conception - prevents 50-70% of NTDs
  • RhoGAM at 28 weeks AND within 72 hours postpartum if Rh negative + baby Rh positive
  • GBS screening 35-37 weeks - if positive, give intrapartum PCN (or alternative if allergic)
  • Glucose screening 24-28 weeks - 1-hour GCT, if abnormal → 3-hour GTT
  • Tdap each pregnancy 27-36 weeks - maternal antibodies protect newborn from pertussis
  • Anatomy ultrasound at 20 weeks - best time to visualize fetal structures
  • cfDNA/NIPT can be done as early as 10 weeks - high sensitivity for trisomy 21
  • Universal HIV, syphilis, HBsAg screening - offer to all, mandatory in many states
  • Hepatitis C screening now universal (ACOG 2021) - high prevalence, vertical transmission risk
  • Postpartum visit at 6 weeks - but earlier contact (3 weeks) recommended by ACOG for high-risk
  • OGTT 6-12 weeks postpartum if GDM - 50% develop T2DM within 5 years
  • Induction recommended by 42 weeks - ↑ stillbirth risk after 42 weeks
