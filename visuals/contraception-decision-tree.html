🌸 Contraception Decision Tree 🌸

Clinical Decision Support for D120

START: Patient Seeking Contraception
❓ Question 1: Does patient have contraindications to estrogen?
(Smoking age >35, history of DVT/PE, stroke, migraines with aura, breast cancer, uncontrolled HTN)

✅ YES - Estrogen Contraindicated

Avoid combined hormonal methods (COCs, patch, ring)

Consider:

Progestin-Only Options

Effectiveness: >99% (perfect use)
  • IUD (Mirena, Skyla, Kyleena): 3-8 years, reversible
  • Nexplanon (implant): 3 years, highly effective
  • Depo-Provera (shot): Every 3 months
  • Mini-pill (norethindrone): Daily pill

⚠️ Considerations:

  • Mini-pill: must take same time daily
  • Depo: bone density concerns, delayed fertility return
  • All: irregular bleeding common initially

Non-Hormonal Options

  • Copper IUD (Paragard): 10 years, hormone-free
  • Barrier methods: Condoms, diaphragm
  • Permanent: Tubal ligation, vasectomy

❌ NO - Can Consider Estrogen-Containing Methods

Proceed to Question 2

❓ Question 2: What is patient's preference for method duration/permanence?

Long-Acting Reversible (LARC)

IUDs

Effectiveness: >99%

✅ Pros:

  • Highly effective
  • Long duration
  • Reversible
  • "Set and forget"

❌ Cons:

  • Insertion procedure
  • Upfront cost
  • Irregular bleeding initially

Hormonal IUD: Lighter/no periods
Copper IUD: Heavier periods, no hormones

Nexplanon (Implant)

Effectiveness: >99%

Single rod in upper arm, 3 years, progestin-only

✅ Pros:

  • Highest effectiveness
  • Quick insertion/removal
  • No estrogen

❌ Cons:

  • Unpredictable bleeding
  • Visible/palpable
  • Arm bruising at insertion

Short-Acting Methods

Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs)

Effectiveness: 91% (typical) / 99% (perfect)

✅ Pros:

  • Regular, predictable cycles
  • Decreased dysmenorrhea
  • Acne improvement
  • Decreased ovarian/endometrial cancer risk
  • Reversible immediately

❌ Cons:

  • Daily compliance required
  • Nausea, headaches possible
  • Drug interactions
  • Estrogen side effects

⚠️ Contraindications:

  • Smoking + age >35
  • History DVT/PE, stroke
  • Migraine with aura
  • Breast cancer
  • Uncontrolled hypertension

Patch (Xulane) / Ring (NuvaRing)

Effectiveness: 91% (typical) / 99% (perfect)

Patch: Change weekly × 3 weeks, 1 week off
Ring: Insert 3 weeks, remove 1 week

✅ Pros:

  • Same benefits as COCs
  • Better compliance (weekly vs daily)
  • Less GI side effects

❌ Cons:

  • Patch: visible, skin irritation
  • Ring: vaginal discomfort
  • Same contraindications as COCs

Depo-Provera (Injectable)

Effectiveness: 94% (typical) / 99% (perfect)

IM injection every 12 weeks, progestin-only

✅ Pros:

  • Private (no visible method)
  • Only 4 visits/year
  • Decreased periods (amenorrhea common)

❌ Cons:

  • Weight gain common
  • Delayed return to fertility (10+ months)
  • Bone density concerns (limit 2 years if possible)
  • Irregular bleeding initially

Barrier Methods

Condoms, Diaphragm, Cervical Cap

Effectiveness: 72-88% (typical)

✅ Pros:

  • STI protection (condoms)
  • No hormones
  • No prescription needed (condoms)
  • Immediate reversibility

❌ Cons:

  • Lower effectiveness
  • User-dependent
  • Must use each time

Permanent Methods

Tubal Ligation / Vasectomy

Effectiveness: >99%

✅ Pros:

  • Permanent solution
  • Highly effective
  • No ongoing cost/compliance

❌ Cons:

  • Surgical procedure
  • NOT easily reversible
  • Must be certain about decision

Best for: Patients/couples certain they do not want (more) children

💡 Clinical Pearls for D120:

  • LARC methods (IUD, implant) have highest continuation and satisfaction rates
  • Typical use effectiveness is more relevant than perfect use for counseling
  • Always screen for contraindications before prescribing estrogen-containing methods (use WHO MEC)
  • Dual protection: Consider condoms + hormonal method for STI + pregnancy prevention
  • Emergency contraception: Discuss as backup option (Plan B, Ella, copper IUD)
  • Patient autonomy: Shared decision-making based on patient values, lifestyle, medical history
