Delirium Subtypes

Hyperactive Delirium (15-20%)

Presentation:

  • Agitation, restlessness
  • Hypervigilance
  • Fast/loud speech
  • Combative, uncooperative
  • Hallucinations prominent
  • Wandering, attempting to leave

Recognition: Easily identified (disruptive)

Hypoactive Delirium (20-25%)

Presentation:

  • Lethargy, withdrawn
  • Decreased activity
  • Slow speech/movements
  • Apathy, somnolence
  • Decreased alertness
  • Quiet, "sleepy"

Recognition: Often MISSED (mistaken for depression/fatigue)

Mixed Delirium (50-60%)

Presentation:

  • Alternates between hyper/hypoactive
  • Fluctuates throughout day
  • Most common type
  • Variable symptoms

Recognition: Variable - may be missed during quiet periods

🚨 Clinical Assessment: CAM (Confusion Assessment Method)

Most widely used tool for delirium diagnosis - requires features 1 AND 2 AND (3 OR 4):

  1. Acute onset and fluctuating course (required)
    • Evidence of acute change in mental status from baseline
    • Does behavior fluctuate during the day?
  2. Inattention (required)
    • Difficulty focusing attention
    • Easily distracted
    • Cannot follow conversation
  3. Disorganized thinking (3 or 4 required)
    • Rambling, irrelevant conversation
    • Illogical flow of ideas
    • Unpredictable switching between subjects
  4. Altered level of consciousness (3 or 4 required)
    • Alert (normal)
    • Vigilant (hyperalert)
    • Lethargic (drowsy, easily aroused)
    • Stupor (difficult to arouse)
    • Coma (unarousable)
    • Anything other than "alert" = abnormal

Management Strategies

🔍 Delirium Management

1. Identify & Treat Underlying Cause

  • Review medications (stop culprits)
  • Check labs: CBC, BMP, glucose, UA/culture, CXR
  • Treat infection, correct metabolic abnormalities
  • Adequate hydration, nutrition
  • Pain management

2. Non-Pharmacologic (FIRST-LINE)

  • Reorientation: clocks, calendars, familiar objects
  • Sensory aids: glasses, hearing aids
  • Sleep hygiene: reduce nighttime interruptions
  • Early mobilization
  • Avoid restraints (increase agitation)
  • Family/sitter presence
  • Minimize room changes

3. Pharmacologic (ONLY if safety risk)

  • Haloperidol 0.5-1 mg (hyperactive delirium with aggression)
  • Atypical antipsychotics: quetiapine, risperidone (lower dose)
  • Use sparingly, lowest dose, short duration
  • Avoid benzodiazepines (worsen delirium) - exception: alcohol withdrawal

🧠 Dementia Management

1. Diagnosis & Evaluation

  • Cognitive screening: MMSE, MoCA, SLUMS
  • Rule out reversible causes: B12, TSH, syphilis
  • Neuroimaging: CT/MRI (stroke, tumor, NPH)
  • Functional assessment: ADLs, IADLs

2. Pharmacologic (LIMITED efficacy)

  • Cholinesterase inhibitors: donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine
    • Mild-moderate Alzheimer's
    • Modest benefit, slows decline
  • Memantine: Moderate-severe Alzheimer's (NMDA antagonist)
  • Avoid antipsychotics unless severe behavioral issues (black box warning)

3. Non-Pharmacologic (ESSENTIAL)

  • Structured routines, familiar environment
  • Cognitive stimulation activities
  • Music therapy, reminiscence therapy
  • Caregiver education & support
  • Safety modifications: remove hazards, wandering alerts
  • Advanced care planning early

😔 Depression Management

1. Screening

  • PHQ-9, Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS)
  • Screen all older adults annually
  • Rule out medical causes: hypothyroid, B12, medications

2. Pharmacologic

  • SSRIs first-line: sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram
    • Start low, go slow
    • Monitor for hyponatremia, fall risk
  • Avoid: TCAs (anticholinergic), paroxetine (anticholinergic)
  • Allow 4-6 weeks for response

3. Non-Pharmacologic

  • Psychotherapy (CBT, IPT)
  • Exercise programs
  • Social engagement, activities
  • Treatment of comorbid conditions
  • ECT (severe, treatment-resistant)

🔄 Delirium Prevention (High-Risk Patients)

HELP (Hospital Elder Life Program) Interventions:

  • Orientation: Reorient frequently, cognitive stimulation
  • Sleep: Non-pharmacologic sleep protocol, reduce nighttime noise/care
  • Mobility: Early mobilization, ambulate 3x/day
  • Vision/Hearing: Ensure glasses, hearing aids available
  • Hydration/Nutrition: Adequate intake, avoid dehydration
  • Medications: Avoid high-risk meds (benzos, anticholinergics)
  • Pain: Adequate management (non-opioid when possible)
  • Bowel/Bladder: Avoid catheters, treat constipation

Reduces delirium incidence by 30-40% in hospitalized elderly

💡 Clinical Pearls for D120:

  • Delirium is a MEDICAL EMERGENCY - associated with ↑ mortality, institutionalization, long-term cognitive decline
  • Hypoactive delirium is often MISSED - don't assume quiet patient is "doing fine"
  • Delirium can UNMASK dementia - cognitive deficits may persist after delirium resolves, revealing underlying dementia
  • UTI does NOT always = delirium - asymptomatic bacteriuria is common in elderly; need other symptoms
  • CAM is highly specific (>95%) - if positive, delirium is very likely present
  • Depression + cognitive complaints = pseudodementia - reversible with antidepressant treatment
  • Key differentiator: ATTENTION - impaired in delirium, intact in dementia/depression
  • Sundowning occurs in BOTH - delirium AND dementia (not specific to either)
  • Don't give benzos for delirium - worsens confusion (exception: alcohol withdrawal)
  • Dementia + acute change = superimposed delirium - common, worsens outcomes
  • Recent onset = NOT dementia - dementia is insidious; sudden confusion = delirium or other acute process
  • "I don't know" answers - classic for depression (aware of deficits); dementia patients confabulate or try hard
