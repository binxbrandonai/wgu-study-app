🌺 Menopause Management Guide 🌺

Comprehensive Study Aid for D120

Menopause Timeline

1

Perimenopause

Timing: Begins 2-8 years before menopause (typically 40s)

Definition: Transition period with irregular cycles, fluctuating hormones

Key sign: Menstrual irregularity (variable cycle length, flow changes)

2

Menopause

Timing: Average age 51 (range 45-55)

Definition: 12 consecutive months without menstruation

Diagnosis: Clinical (retrospective), FSH >30 mIU/mL if uncertain

3

Postmenopause

Timing: Begins after 12 months amenorrhea, continues for life

Focus: Long-term health maintenance, chronic disease prevention

Hormone levels: Low estrogen permanently

The Three Phases

Perimenopause

📅 Timeline

2-8 years before menopause

🔬 Physiology

  • Ovarian function declining
  • Fluctuating estrogen/progesterone
  • Irregular ovulation
  • ↑ FSH levels (variable)

📋 Signs

  • Irregular periods (shorter/longer cycles)
  • Variable flow (heavier/lighter)
  • Vasomotor symptoms begin
  • Mood changes
  • Sleep disturbances

⚠️ Important

  • Still fertile! Contraception needed
  • Pregnancy still possible

Menopause

📅 Timeline

Specific point in time

🔬 Definition

  • 12 consecutive months without period
  • Diagnosed retrospectively
  • Average age: 51 years
  • Premature: before age 40
  • Early: age 40-45

📋 Diagnosis

  • Primarily clinical
  • FSH >30 mIU/mL (if needed)
  • Low estradiol
  • History of amenorrhea ×12 months

🩺 Assessment

  • Rule out other causes of amenorrhea
  • Pregnancy test if uncertain
  • Thyroid screening

Postmenopause

📅 Timeline

After 12 months amenorrhea, lifelong

🔬 Physiology

  • Permanently low estrogen
  • Ovarian hormone production ceased
  • Some estrogen from adipose tissue

⚠️ Health Risks

  • ↑ Cardiovascular disease
  • ↑ Osteoporosis/fractures
  • Urogenital atrophy
  • Cognitive changes
  • Weight gain (↓ metabolism)

🎯 Focus

  • Chronic disease prevention
  • Bone health monitoring
  • Cardiovascular risk reduction
  • Quality of life maintenance

Common Symptoms

🔥 Vasomotor Symptoms

  • Hot flashes: Sudden warmth, sweating, flushing (70-80% of women)
  • Night sweats: Disrupts sleep
  • Duration: Average 7.4 years (can be >10 years)
  • Triggers: Stress, heat, spicy foods, caffeine, alcohol

🧠 Neuropsychiatric

  • Mood changes, irritability
  • Anxiety, depression
  • Memory/concentration issues ("brain fog")
  • Sleep disturbances, insomnia

🩺 Urogenital Atrophy

  • Vaginal dryness, dyspareunia
  • Vaginal itching, burning
  • Urinary urgency, frequency
  • Recurrent UTIs
  • Stress incontinence

💪 Metabolic/Physical

  • Weight gain (especially abdominal)
  • Decreased muscle mass
  • Joint aches, stiffness
  • Skin/hair changes (dryness, thinning)
  • Decreased libido

Treatment Options

🌟 Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)

Systemic Estrogen

Forms: Oral, transdermal (patch, gel, spray)

Indications: Moderate-severe vasomotor symptoms, osteoporosis prevention

Benefits:

  • ↓ Hot flashes/night sweats (most effective)
  • ↓ Urogenital atrophy
  • ↓ Bone loss, fracture risk
  • Improved sleep, mood, QOL

Notes:

  • Must add progestin if uterus intact (endometrial protection)
  • Use lowest effective dose
  • Transdermal preferred if VTE risk

Progestin (if uterus present)

Purpose: Prevent endometrial hyperplasia/cancer

Options:

  • Continuous combined (daily estrogen + progestin)
  • Cyclic (progestin 12-14 days/month)
  • Mirena IUD (local progestin)

NOT needed: If hysterectomy (estrogen alone)

Local Vaginal Estrogen

Forms: Cream, tablet, ring

Indications: Urogenital symptoms ONLY (no vasomotor)

Benefits:

  • Treats vaginal dryness, dyspareunia
  • ↓ Recurrent UTIs
  • Low systemic absorption
  • Safe long-term use

Note: No progestin needed (minimal systemic absorption)

Duration & Monitoring

Current guidelines:

  • No arbitrary time limit
  • Continue if benefits outweigh risks
  • Reassess annually
  • Individualized decision

Monitoring:

  • Annual exam, symptom assessment
  • Mammogram as per guidelines
  • Report any vaginal bleeding

🚫 HRT Contraindications (Absolute)

  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding
  • Breast cancer (current or history)
  • Endometrial cancer (current)
  • Active VTE/PE or history
  • Active liver disease
  • Known thrombophilia
  • CAD, stroke (controversial - relative contraindication)

⚠️ Risks of HRT (especially if started >10 years post-menopause or age >60):

  • ↑ VTE risk (especially oral estrogen)
  • ↑ Stroke risk (small absolute increase)
  • ↑ Breast cancer risk with long-term use (>5 years combined HRT)
  • ↑ Gallbladder disease

💡 Window of opportunity: HRT safest when started within 10 years of menopause onset or before age 60

Non-Hormonal Treatments

SSRIs/SNRIs

Examples: Paroxetine, venlafaxine, escitalopram

Effectiveness: Reduce hot flashes 50-65%

Best for: Contraindications to HRT, mood symptoms

Note: Paroxetine FDA-approved for hot flashes

Gabapentin

Effectiveness: Reduces hot flashes, improves sleep

Dose: 300-900 mg/day

Side effects: Drowsiness, dizziness

Best for: Night sweats, sleep disturbance

Lifestyle Modifications

  • Layer clothing, keep cool
  • Avoid triggers (heat, spice, alcohol, caffeine)
  • Regular exercise
  • Stress reduction, mindfulness
  • Adequate sleep hygiene
  • Healthy diet, weight management

Other Options

  • Clonidine: Modest benefit for hot flashes
  • Vaginal moisturizers/lubricants: For dryness (non-hormonal)
  • CBT: For mood, sleep, hot flash distress
  • Acupuncture: Some evidence for vasomotor symptoms

Postmenopausal Screening & Prevention

Osteoporosis ↓ Estrogen → ↓ bone density DEXA scan at age 65 (earlier if risk factors) • Calcium 1200 mg/day
• Vitamin D 800-1000 IU/day
• Weight-bearing exercise
• Bisphosphonates if osteoporosis
Cardiovascular Disease ↑ Risk after menopause (loss of estrogen protection) BP, lipids, diabetes screening • Control HTN, lipids, diabetes
• Healthy diet (Mediterranean)
• Regular exercise
• Smoking cessation
• Maintain healthy weight

• Clinical breast exam
• Know family history
Colorectal Cancer ↑ Risk with age Colonoscopy every 10 years starting age 45-50 • FIT test annually (alternative)

• Transvaginal ultrasound
• Progestin if on estrogen (if uterus)

💡 Clinical Pearls for D120:

  • Menopause diagnosis: Primarily clinical - 12 months amenorrhea. Don't routinely check FSH in women >45 with typical symptoms
  • HRT timing matters: Safest/most beneficial if started within 10 years of menopause onset ("window of opportunity")
  • Breast cancer risk: Combined estrogen+progestin >5 years ↑ risk. Estrogen-only (post-hysterectomy) may actually ↓ risk
  • Local vs systemic estrogen: If ONLY urogenital symptoms → local vaginal estrogen (no systemic risks, no progestin needed)
  • Contraception in perimenopause: Continue until 12 months amenorrhea if age >50, or 24 months if age <50
  • Postmenopausal bleeding: ALWAYS investigate (endometrial cancer until proven otherwise) - not normal!
  • SSRIs/SNRIs: Good alternative if HRT contraindicated - also help mood symptoms
  • Bone health: All postmenopausal women need calcium/vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise
  • Shared decision-making: HRT decision based on individual symptoms, risks, preferences - no "one size fits all"
  • Premature menopause (age <40): Usually recommend HRT until natural menopause age (~51) to prevent long-term sequelae
